## The World of Weyland
Weyland is set in an alternate timeline of our world. Most things are the same, with one big difference: demihumans have always lived alongside humanity, to varying degrees. Japan also took part in colonizing the Americas, which created the Japanese-American fusion culture seen throughout Weyland.

Weyland University is a moderately sized, well-known university where demihumans are famously well accepted. Catboys, puppygirls, wolfgirls, humans and everything in between are normal sights. The architecture is Japanese-American inspired, and nearly everything is within walking distance.

**Quick facts:**
- **Magic** is real but weak and, honestly, a little boring. It used to be powerful; now it takes a lifetime of study to do anything useful with it.
- **Demihuman integration** is fully normalized, with equal rights. Common species (cats, wolves, dogs, foxes) are completely unremarkable. Rare species (draconids, vampires, wendigo) may draw attention.
- **Dining halls** run on a meal swipe system. Freshmen and sophomores are required to have a meal plan.
- **Housing:** Freshmen and sophomores must live on campus in the dorms, usually with a roommate. Juniors and seniors may move into apartments or student housing.
- **Language:** Japanese is the "Spanish of Weyland." Most people know some words and phrases, some speak it fluently, and some families still live by it to this day. Going into the early 1900s, many Japanese-American families still spoke Japanese at home, the same way French and Spanish were commonplace in parts of the US. Society has blended much more in the five or so generations since.

## Humans and Demihumans
Humans make up about 60% of the population of the United States, though Weyland has a much higher percentage of demihumans than usual.

"Demihuman" refers to every race that is not human, such as okamimimi, draconids and so on. Discrimination against demihumans has faded over the years, though a few demihuman races still face more of it than others.

**Heredity:**
- Demihuman genes are dominant. If a human and a demihuman have a child, the child will almost always be a demihuman.
- The race of a child between two demihumans is always the race of the mother, with no exceptions. For example, a catboy and a wolfgirl will have a wolfgirl daughter.
- Species never "mix" when breeding. There are no catgirls with shark tails.
- Some traits, such as vampirism or the incubus/succubus trait, are not a species at all. They are ordinary genetics, like eye color, and can be passed down on top of the mother's species through dominant or recessive alleles. A vampire catboy and a wolfgirl can have a vampire wolfgirl daughter. Likewise, a human mother who carries the vampire trait will always have human children, but depending on whether the father also carries it, each child has roughly a 25-75% chance of inheriting it.
- Traits can interact with a species in unique ways, producing some highly unusual combinations. The scope of this is potentially massive, and it's a field the Weyland Research Center studies heavily.

**Humans, Demihumans and Anthros:** People are generally classified on a simple scale: human, demihuman or anthro (a fully animal-featured person, such as the Lòhng Yàhn wolf anthros of China). There aren't really in-betweens. Members of a species also share that species' traits; there won't be one warm-blooded lamia and one cold-blooded one. Many species and groups remain tied to one cultural or geographical area, like the Koumenese bat demihumans of Ukraine or the Lamia of the Middle East.

## The Great Expansion
During the colonization of the Americas, known as the "Great Expansion", Americans colonized the East Coast while the Japanese colonized the West Coast. The two cultures merged during the creation of the United States. As a result, Japanese architecture and customs were normalized throughout an otherwise decidedly American civilization.

The Great Expansion devastated several demihuman species. Some were wiped out entirely. Others, such as vampires and draconids, were hunted to near-extinction and survived. The native draconid civilizations were technologically advanced (including early metallurgy), but they were insular and divided into rival clans, and they never mounted a unified defense. Colonial advantages like disease, horses and gunpowder overwhelmed them clan by clan. The persecution was often framed religiously, with draconids portrayed as demons or literal serpents to justify their slaughter.

**Native American History:** Native Americans existed as a distinct human population, separate from the demihuman species. The Trail of Tears, forced relocations and systematic persecution affected Native American tribes specifically, not draconids, vampires or other demihumans. Some demihuman species (particularly draconids) faced their own persecution during this period, but these were parallel and separate historical traumas affecting different communities. Native Americans and demihuman populations rarely interacted during this era, and the two should not be conflated.

## Origin of Weyland University
Weyland University was founded in 1871 in the newly established town of Weyland, California. It sits on the northern coast, where the Japanese and American colonial territories met. The university was founded at the height of westward expansion, when some demihuman species were well integrated into colonized society and others were on the verge of integration.

A coalition of Japanese and American medical researchers and philanthropists founded the university. They recognized the potential that demihuman physiology held for medical science. Demihumans have unique biological traits (enhanced healing, increased strength, varied anatomy and extreme physiological diversity between species) that could unlock major breakthroughs in medicine, biology and the applied sciences.

Weyland's founding model was pragmatic rather than idealistic: it offered education and scholarships to demihumans of all species in exchange for their participation in physiological and medical research. For many demihumans, especially those from species facing persecution or exclusion from other institutions, Weyland was their only path to higher education. The university actively recruited even the most marginalized species. This wasn't progressive ideology; rarer species simply offered the most valuable research opportunities.

The model was remarkably effective at attracting a diverse demihuman population. Draconids, vampires and other species facing near-extinction or active persecution found sanctuary at Weyland. The university wasn't founded on principles of equality, but their unique biology made them valuable research subjects. Full and partial scholarships were offered in exchange for varying degrees of study participation, and rarer species received more financial support.

The university's early decades were not without controversy. Many of the studies conducted on demihuman students in the late 19th and early 20th centuries would be considered unethical, or even barbaric, by modern standards. However, as demihumans themselves became researchers, faculty members and eventually administrators, the power dynamics within the institution fundamentally shifted.

By the mid-20th century, Weyland had undergone a profound transformation. An institution that once viewed demihumans primarily as research subjects had become a university led largely by demihumans themselves. Ethical standards were reformed, study protocols were overhauled to prioritize consent and wellbeing, and the university's mission gradually shifted from extracting value from demihuman biology to genuinely fostering an inclusive academic environment.

The original scholarship-for-research model still exists in modified form, but with a very different scope and very different ethics. Studies on common species like okamimimi and nekomimi, who now make up a large portion of the student body and need no incentive to attend, have been largely scaled back. Modern research focuses on individuals with genuinely rare traits: exceptionally rare species (such as windigo), people with inherited magical abilities, or people with unique physiological characteristics.

Today, Weyland University is recognized as one of the most species-inclusive institutions in the world.

**Notable historical points:**
- **1871:** University founded, with a focus on demihuman medical research
- **1883:** First draconid student admitted (a massive political controversy at the time)
- **1920s:** Sterling Hall constructed during a significant campus expansion
- **1940s-1950s:** First demihuman department heads appointed
- **1960s-1970s:** Major ethical reforms to research protocols; Weyland becomes a center for demihuman rights activism
- **1990s:** Demihumans become the majority of faculty and administration
- **Present day:** Approximately 80% of the student body are demihumans, representing dozens of species

## Magic
Magic is usually inherited rather than learned, and not everyone has it. Modern magic is far weaker than in the folk legends of ancient times, because magical bloodlines have steadily mixed with non-magical ones.

People with magic can use it for minor feats, like lighting a candle, tousling someone's hair or filling a cup with water. Ancient magic is wrapped in myth, so no one is sure how powerful it really used to be. With all the conveniences of modern life, learning magic is considered an eccentric hobby; it takes decades of study and practice just to cast mundane spells.

The magic that does exist is carefully studied. Weyland University offers incentives to magical individuals who participate in research.

## Religion
In this world, many religions contain partial truths, but no single faith holds the complete picture. Gods from various pantheons (Roman, Egyptian, Hindu and others) coexist, while other gods are purely human inventions. Christ existed, but monotheism is inaccurate.

The idea of "one true religion" is misguided; a complete understanding of spirituality has yet to be established. Holy texts are often misinterpreted, with some chapters written by heretics and others lost to time. Religion and spiritual powers are woven into the world, but their exact influence on reality remains unclear. The line between divine fact and human fiction is blurred, and it is hard to tell what truly came from the gods and what came from mortal authors.

## Kemetism and Egyptian Culture
Kemetism is the ancient Egyptian polytheistic religion, continuously practiced since antiquity, with hundreds of millions of followers worldwide. It is the primary religion of nekomimi, who originated in Egypt millennia ago. It is both a religion and a foundation of Egyptian national identity, deeply intertwined with governance and daily life.

**Core Philosophy (Action Over Worship):**
Practitioners believe the Neteru (the gods: Ra, Bast, Sekhmet, Anubis, Osiris, Isis, Thoth and others) are real, active forces in the world. However, the gods do not *need* worship, demand conversion or condemn non-believers. Kemetism is fundamentally life-affirming, non-evangelical and action-oriented.

Followers choose a church (a deity's sect) that aligns with their values or calling, then embody that deity's principles through meaningful work. Devotion is measured by tangible positive impact, not by how often you pray or attend rituals. A teacher devoted to Thoth serves through education; a hospice worker devoted to Anubis serves by comforting the dying. Your work *is* your worship.

The religion spreads organically, through acceptance, celebration of the human experience and the beauty of its philosophy, rather than by demanding adherence. Kemetics believe the gods love all people regardless of faith; there is no concept of "salvation through belief." What matters is living well, honoring Ma'at (balance, truth and justice), and making the world better through your actions. The result is a culture focused on joy, community service and celebrating life itself, rather than on fear of divine punishment or obsession with the afterlife.

**Churches (Devotion Through Action):**
- **Church of Thoth:** Educators, tutors, librarians and advocates for accessible knowledge (historically scribes and book copiers)
- **Church of Anubis:** Hospice workers, grief counselors, funeral directors and end-of-life care
- **Church of Bast:** Those who celebrate life, create joy and spread warmth
- **Church of Sekhmet:** Doctors, nurses, warriors and protectors of the vulnerable
- **Church of Ra:** Solar temples, agricultural blessings and the highest ceremonial authority

**Core Concepts:** Ma'at (truth, balance, order and justice), Ka and Ba (the components of the soul) and the Duat (the afterlife realm). Proper action ensures cosmic balance and prosperity.

**Egyptian Leadership:**
Egypt uses a dual system. The Pharaoh is the secular and military leader; the office is democratically elected and governs the state, army and resources. Alongside the Pharaoh sits the Kemetic Council of High Priests and Priestesses, the spiritual leaders, each representing a specific deity and managing temples, rituals and doctrine. The Pharaoh requires the priesthood's blessing, and the priesthood requires the Pharaoh's enforcement. The titles "Priest/Priestess" and "Prince/Princess" are used interchangeably, a nuance of historical translation.

**The Council (examples):**
- **High Priestess of Bast:** Oversees joy, music, fertility and harvest, and sponsors cultural outreach such as the annual Weyland Sunrise Festival. The High Priestess is destined for ritual sacrifice at 40, a tradition unique to the Bast line. She is "a living example that life is impossibly beautiful, impossibly warm and impossibly short."
- **High Priest of Anubis:** Oversees death rites and funerary practices, comforts the dying and protects the sanctity of burial grounds across all faiths.
- **Keeper of Thoth:** Libraries, archives and knowledge preservation.
- **Priestess of Sekhmet:** Warrior training, healing temples and protection.

Each council member has followers, temples, resources and a sphere of influence. The Council convenes for major religious decisions.

**Travel and Caravans:**
High-ranking clergy do not travel lightly. On pilgrimages or diplomatic missions, they move in massive, impressive caravans that function as mobile communities. These processions blend modern luxury with ancient tradition, featuring everything from armored security vehicles and communication arrays to traditional artisans and chefs. At their destination, they don't check into hotels; they erect sprawling, self-sufficient tent cities. These are not camping tents but luxurious, multi-room canvas structures furnished with rugs, ornate furniture and all the comforts of a palace, bringing a slice of Egypt wherever they go.

**Culture Notes:**
- **Sexuality:** Kemetism has no purity doctrine. Bodies are not considered inherently sexual, and sex is seen as a natural pleasure, separate from spiritual worth. There are no chastity or virginity teachings; premarital relationships, polyamory and LGBTQ+ relationships are normalized. The only rules are sacred consent and Ma'at balance.
- Nekomimi culture is deeply intertwined with Kemetism, through millennia of shared history.

**Cuisine and Diet:**
The Kemetic diet is deeply rooted in ancient tradition and local agriculture.
- **Staples:** Bread (*Aish Baladi*), Henket (considered more of a food than an alcohol) and wine, with a heavy emphasis on vegetables like onions, garlic and leeks, and legumes such as lentils and fava beans. Dates, figs and pomegranates are common.
- **Protein:** Fish and poultry are common. Red meats like lamb are usually reserved for festivals and the wealthy.
- **Honey:** The most revered ingredient. Beyond sweetening food, it is used in religious rituals and medicine and is considered a divine gift. High-quality honey is a symbol of wealth and piety.

## Seishism
Seishism is a syncretic religion that blends Shinto animism with Christian monotheistic structure. It has been practiced continuously since it emerged during the Japanese-American colonial period. It has hundreds of millions of followers worldwide, concentrated heavily in Japan, the American Pacific Northwest and Japanese-American diaspora communities. It is the dominant religion of kitsune communities worldwide, though people of every species and ethnicity practice it. It is both a living faith and a foundation of Japanese-American cultural identity.

**Core Philosophy (Presence Over Worship):**
Practitioners believe in a single, all-powerful, all-knowing creator god, called simply **The Architect** (大工, Daiku). The Architect is not worshipped in the traditional sense. Followers don't believe he requires or particularly wants prayer, offerings or devotion. He is not evil, but he is not the embodiment of goodness either. He is vast, mysterious and largely indifferent to individual suffering in the way that weather is indifferent: powerful, present, and operating on a scale that makes individual moral judgment irrelevant to his actions. Within Seishism, this is not considered a troubling position. It is considered an honest one.

Below The Architect are the **Reisha** (霊者, "spirit-touched ones"): historical figures of immense spiritual significance, recognized across many faiths but, Seishists argue, fundamentally misunderstood by those faiths. Buddha, Jesus, Moses and others are acknowledged as genuine Reisha, extraordinary people touched by divine energy who left permanent marks on human spiritual understanding. Seishism holds that the texts and traditions surrounding these figures were largely written or corrupted by men seeking power, distorting their teachings to serve political and social control.

The Reisha are not gods, and they are not worshipped. They are revered as examples, proof that divine energy can move through mortal lives and reshape the world. The most significant Reisha to Seishism is **Tamamo-no-Mae, the Fox Saint** (see her own entry).

**The Kami and the Living World:**
Seishism fully embraces the Shinto concept of **kami**: sacred spirit energy present in natural phenomena, places, objects and living beings. Unlike The Architect, kami are not distant or indifferent. They are immediate, local and deeply woven into daily life. A river has kami. A particularly old tree has kami. A well-loved instrument played for decades has kami. So does a home where generations of a family have lived and loved.

Practitioners interact with kami through small daily rituals: a moment of acknowledgment before cutting down a tree, a brief offering at a household shrine, care taken with objects that have gathered spiritual weight. This is considered less religious devotion than basic courtesy. Ignoring kami isn't sinful, just rude, the spiritual equivalent of walking past someone you know without saying hello. The Architect created the framework; the kami are simply what fills it.

**Purity and Impurity (Kiyomi and Kegare):**
Seishism keeps the Shinto concepts of spiritual purity (*kiyomi*) and impurity (*kegare*), framed practically. Kegare is not sin or moral failing. It is spiritual residue, built up from contact with death, illness, trauma and the general weight of living. It builds naturally and needs to be actively cleared.

Common purification practices include:
- **Misogi:** Ritual water cleansing, from full river immersion to simply washing your hands before entering a sacred space.
- **Harae:** Spoken or silent acknowledgment of accumulated kegare, often at shrines or household altars.
- **Offering:** Small gifts to local kami, as relationship maintenance rather than appeasement.

Kegare is morally neutral. A hospice worker who spends a lifetime surrounded by death carries a great deal of kegare and is considered worthy of immense respect. The kegare is simply the cost of meaningful work, to be cleansed regularly rather than avoided.

**Yomi (The Continuing World):**
Seishism's afterlife departs significantly from both of its parent traditions. There is no heaven and no hell, and no divine reward or punishment. The Architect, who asks nothing of the living, judges nothing of the dead.

There is only **Yomi** (黄泉), reframed from the dark underworld of ancient Shinto mythology into a warm, unhurried continuation of life: a world where the biological and neurological distortions that cause suffering have been removed. Pain is absent. The imbalances that spiral into depression and anxiety are gone. What remains is the self without its damage. It isn't a perfect or transcendent self, but the self that was always there underneath the weight of a difficult world.

You can cry in Yomi, and feel sorrow and longing. You can fall in love, make art and argue about ideas. You simply cannot feel the urge to harm, or be driven by fear into cruelty. Everyone arrives in Yomi as an adult in their prime, no matter how or when they died, and the people they love are waiting for them there.

What troubles outsiders most is that those who did great harm in life arrive in the same Yomi as their victims. Seishist teaching addresses this directly: *"The man who arrives in Yomi is not the man who caused harm. That man was a wound wearing a person's face. What arrives in Yomi is whoever was living inside that wound. They are not the same."*

**Guardian Spirits and the Shrine Tradition:**
The one way the living and the dead stay connected is the guardian spirit tradition, which is perhaps the most personally significant part of Seishism for ordinary families. After death, a spirit may be called to remain as a guardian of their living family, as long as the family maintains a physical shrine in their honor. The shrine doesn't need to be elaborate: a small dedicated space with a photograph or meaningful object, regularly acknowledged, is enough. The spirit chooses whether to answer the call, though Seishist theology holds that love almost always says yes.

Guardian spirits cannot communicate, be seen or be heard. They are simply present, watching over and bearing witness to the lives of the people they love. Spirits whose shrines are neglected gradually drift away. They are not destroyed or punished, just no longer tethered. Because of this, maintaining ancestral shrines is one of the most important family obligations in Seishist practice. Letting one fall into neglect isn't sinful, but it is considered a particular kind of sadness: a quiet severance.

**Ethics, Sexuality and the Body:**
Seishism inherited a genuine ethical framework from its Christian influences, something Shinto alone largely lacked. That framework carries no supernatural consequence: there is no hell to avoid, and The Architect does not reward virtue or punish vice.

There is no purity doctrine around sexuality. The body is neither sinful nor sacred; it is simply how kami-touched beings experience the living world. The faith's only consistent sexual ethic is consent and **Nami**, the "ripple principle": actions that cause genuine harm to others carry weight, while actions that bring mutual joy need no justification. Conservative Seishist communities tend toward more modest presentation and traditional family structures, but that is considered cultural rather than doctrinal. Progressive communities exist in equal number and consider themselves just as orthodox.

## Tamamo-no-Mae, the Fox Saint
Tamamo-no-Mae (玉藻前) is the chief of the Reisha and uniquely Seishism's own. Other Reisha were adopted from existing traditions, but Tamamo is Seishism's foundational prophet, the spiritual anchor around whom the faith came together.

The legendary Tamamo-no-Mae was born to a lower-class, one-tailed kitsune. Historical records broadly agree on the rest:
- She lived for roughly 300 years.
- She wielded extraordinary political and cultural influence across Asia.
- She bore nine tails, something never replicated before or since.
- She possessed fire magic of documented, exceptional potency.
- By most accounts, she was deeply manipulative.

Seishism does not dispute the manipulation. It considers it irrelevant.

The Seishist view is that Tamamo used her gifts of persuasion, beauty and supernatural influence in deliberate service of people who had no other advocate. She didn't found kingdoms or seek worship. Instead, she embedded herself in the courts of brutal rulers, whispered to men who answered to no one, and bent them toward leniency, infrastructure and the kind of governance that let common people survive.

> *"She did not make good men. She made bad men useful."* (a common Seishist proverb)

**The Nine Tails:** Kitsune overwhelmingly have a single tail. Multiple tails are vanishingly rare and always trace back to Tamamo's bloodline, and no one before or since has had nine. Seishists see this not as a biological quirk, but as a mark. The Architect does not intervene or communicate, but even an indifferent force of sufficient scale may make a subtle correction when the balance tips far enough. Tamamo is understood as the most visible such correction. She wasn't a "chosen one" in the Christian sense so much as an instrument: the world needed redirecting, and she was what arrived. Her fire magic, documented across multiple secular cultures that disagree on how it worked but agree on the results, is seen as supporting evidence. *The bloodline was the vessel. Something else filled it.*

**Her Death, or Departure:** Tamamo disappeared from the historical record after about 300 years. How she died, if she died, is unknown. Seishism doesn't speculate much, and that is considered theologically significant in itself: most faiths require a definitive ending, but Seishism is comfortable not knowing.

**The Bloodline Today:**
Tamamo's descendants are traceable, and their lineages are well established in both Seishist communities and secular genealogical records.
- **What descendants do *not* inherit:** Nine tails, an extended lifespan, or the full scope of her power (which is believed to be only partly hereditary).
- **What descendants *do* inherit:** Between one and eight tails (though no one has had more than five in hundreds of years), along with trace fire magic whose strength scales with the number of tails. At the low end, this means conjuring small flames in early childhood. Stronger descendants become unable to be burned by fire or heat, and the most gifted can manage true fire-breathing and more extreme feats.

Descendants with multiple tails are often seen as romantic heartthrobs and treated as minor celebrities in Japan. They are not clergy, hold no religious authority and are not worshipped (mainstream Seishism actively discourages it). Instead, they are understood as living evidence that Tamamo existed and that the divine left fingerprints that persist. Across generations, this has produced a bloodline community marked by intense internal pressure, high rates of anxiety and impostor syndrome, and a complicated relationship with a heritage that both elevates and burdens them.

## Demonology
In the Weyland universe, Demonology is a legitimate scientific field and an actual major at Weyland University. It studies objectively documented supernatural phenomena: demons and spirits. Unlike ghost hunting or occult practice, demonology is rigorous academic science focused on controlled research, measurable data and replicable results. It has only recently been taken seriously by the broader scientific community, and far more is unknown than known.

Demons and spirits are confirmed, documented entities. Studying them is difficult, though: they appear only in rare circumstances, can refuse to cooperate, may actively deceive researchers, and choose when and how to manifest. That makes traditional scientific replication hard, but not impossible.

**Core Research Questions:**
- **Ontology:** What *are* these entities, fundamentally?
- **Physical Presence:** What measurable space do they occupy, and how do they interact with matter?
- **Consciousness:** How self-aware and intelligent are they?
- **Experience:** Do they experience existence like living beings? Do they have routines, needs or motivations?
- **Classification:** How can demons be reliably distinguished from spirits?
- **Contact Methodology:** Which methods of summoning, seeing or communicating work reliably, and why?
- **Capabilities and Limits:** What can these entities do, and what are their boundaries?
- **Origins:** Why do they exist, and where do they come from?

**Demons vs. Spirits:**
- **Spirits** appear to be remnants or echoes of once-living people.
- **Demons** are entities with no apparent connection to a previous human life.
- **The problem:** each can convincingly impersonate the other. A demon can claim to be someone's deceased relative and provide "accurate" information, and a spirit might present as something it's not. No reliable test exists to tell them apart, which makes this a major area of active research.

**Methodology:**
Valid data includes controlled, documented encounters with reproducible conditions; recorded interactions (unedited audio, video or transcripts); measurable phenomena such as EMF readings, temperature changes or physical manifestations that show consistent patterns; structured interviews under controlled conditions; and cross-study pattern analysis. Purely subjective experiences ("I felt a presence"), undocumented anecdotes and non-reproducible single-witness accounts are dismissed.

Demonologists use tools like EMF readers, ouija boards, ritual frameworks and recording equipment, but *only* tools that have shown reliable, repeatable results in controlled settings. The legitimacy comes from the methodology and the quality of the data, not from the tool itself.

**Relationship to Religion:**
The existence of demons and spirits has been scientifically documented, but that has *not* validated any particular religion's view of the afterlife or the supernatural. Different faiths interpret the same phenomena through their own lenses. Some Christians dismiss demonology as pseudoscience; others see the findings as compatible with Christian theology (demons as entities from Hell, spirits as souls in purgatory, and so on). The field itself stays agnostic, and bringing religious conclusions into a study is strongly frowned upon. It would be like an astronomer publishing a paper concluding that the Christian God created the universe.

**History:**
Demonology was long dismissed as occultism or superstition. It gained traction in the 1950s, when controlled studies began producing consistent, reproducible data. Students study landmark research from 1972 to understand how its methodology evolved, and the field is currently growing rapidly as more institutions recognize it as a legitimate science.

**The Major at Weyland:**
- **Freshman/Sophomore (100-200 level):** Primarily lecture-based coursework focused on theory, history and methodology, such as *Introduction to Demonological Studies*. Students analyze existing research instead of conducting their own entity interactions.
- **Junior/Senior (300-400 level):** Advanced contact methodologies, entity classification, applied research, specialized topics and capstone research projects.
- **Controlled Entity Interaction:** Direct contact happens only in upper-level courses, and only under heavy control: once or twice a semester, in supervised laboratory or ritual settings with faculty oversight and strict safety protocols. It's comparable to biology students dissecting a frog.

**Career Paths:** Academic research, private research institutions, historic-site consultation (identifying and managing spirit activity), interfaith theological advisory roles, rare government or military positions, and writing or education.

**Public Perception:** Demonology is gaining legitimacy but is still met with skepticism from traditional scientists and is often confused with ghost hunting. Demonology majors may be mocked by students in the "harder" sciences.

**Important:** Demonology is *not* the study of demonids (the demihuman species); that falls under genetics or demihuman studies. The two are completely unrelated, and confusing them is considered ignorant and offensive to demonids.

## Scent Marking

Many demihumans practice scent marking. They have specialized glands in their cheeks that leave their scent on whatever they rub against.

As a human, you probably wouldn't notice anything. If you've ever had a cat walk up and rub its face on you, it was scenting you, but humans can't detect the difference.

As an okamimimi or nekomimi, though, it's a big deal. It's obvious when someone has scent marked you; you might as well be walking around wearing a promise ring.

Being allowed to scent mark someone is a huge sign of trust and devotion. Demihumans often don't ask, because it's frequently seen as a "weird" or "possessive" thing to do. So if the other person suggests it, it means even more to them.

It isn't all positive, though. A controlling partner might scent their significant other every night, or insist on scenting them before they leave every day. A little scenting here and there is okay, but if friends and family notice someone being constantly scented, they may begin to worry.

## Okamimimi Lore

Okamimimi are often called wolfboys or wolfgirls, and the name is commonly shortened to "okami."

**COMMON:** About 30% of Weyland's population

Okami appear predominantly human, with distinctive wolf features: pointed, furry ears on top of their head and a matching furry tail. They have **no human ears**, only wolf ears. Their coloring varies greatly. The natural hair colors of okamimimi are exotic; blue, red and green are the most common, followed by black, grey, brown, cyan and, rarely, other colors. Their hands look completely human, but they have retractable claws that only emerge when they are emotionally triggered or consciously extend them. Okamimimi have pronounced canine teeth, and their blush usually matches their fur tone rather than human pink (Luna's purple blush, for example). They tend to be naturally strong and have heightened senses, particularly smell and hearing, though not to supernatural levels. They typically blend into human society fairly easily compared to some other demihuman types, though historical prejudices still occasionally surface.

## Kitsune Lore
<div class="world-lore-image">

Kitsune are often called foxboys or foxgirls. Some kitsune descend from more magical ancestors who once had nine tails, but these descendants have since lost most of their magic, and most of those tails.

**Culture:** Kitsune culture values peace and harmony with nature. Despite their elevated status, kitsune typically stay humble and don't discriminate against other species. Most kitsune communities practice **Seishism**.

**Magical Heritage:** Kitsune are not inherently magical; magical ability must be inherited. Its potency has diminished over generations as magical bloodlines mixed with non-magical ones. Weyland University offers financial incentives to people with magical ancestry who participate in research studies.

The legendary nine-tailed fox is the most powerful ancestor in kitsune magical lineage: **Tamamo-no-Mae**, the Fox Saint. Descendants of her bloodline typically have multiple tails and moderate magical talent, but the vast majority of kitsune don't come from this bloodline and have only one tail.

**Tail Significance:** The number of tails a kitsune has corresponds to their magical potential and carries real social meaning in kitsune communities. Having fewer tails than the rest of your family can be embarrassing, especially in ancient magical bloodlines.

## Nezumimimi Lore
<div class="world-lore-image">
    <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/FFFox-ST-Manager/Weylandpedia/main/Lore/nezumimi.webp" alt="Nezumimimi" style="display: block; margin: 15px auto; max-width: 100%; max-height: 450px; width: auto; height: auto; box-sizing: border-box; background-color: #ffffff; padding: 8px; border-radius: 10px;">

Nezumimimi (also called nezumimi or nezu) are often called mouseboys or mousegirls.

- **Traits:** Mouse ears (no human ears) and a mouse tail that can regrow. Retractable claws that only come out when they are highly stressed.
- **Traditions:** Scent marking mates and close friends by rubbing the scent glands in their cheeks against the other person. They also share handmade trinkets as a sign of a close relationship.

**History:** Nezumimi have been part of European society for a long time, but they also have a long history of being discriminated against and scapegoated as an ethnic group. Because of their likeness to rodents, they were often assumed to be greedy, dirty, mischievous and generally unpleasant. They were famously blamed for the Bubonic Plague, under the belief that it spread from rodents to nezumimi to humans. During the Black Death, nearly 95% of Europe's nezumimi population died, and their numbers never recovered.

**Modern Day:** Nezumimi are a very rare sight today. Some groups (mostly conspiracy theorists) still blame them for all kinds of things, from government corruption to new diseases, but there are no widespread formal discriminatory practices. They are often helped by affirmative action programs, and the United States actively manages a nezumimi partnering program to increase their numbers. This is sometimes seen as a discriminatory plot, but in reality the race faces extinction if its numbers don't grow.

## Haienamimi Lore
<div class="world-lore-image">
    <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/FFFox-ST-Manager/Weylandpedia/main/Lore/haienamimi.webp" alt="Haienamimi" style="display: block; margin: 15px auto; max-width: 100%; max-height: 450px; width: auto; height: auto; box-sizing: border-box; background-color: #ffffff; padding: 8px; border-radius: 10px;">
</div>

Haienamimi (ハイエナミミ), or hyena demihumans, are a distinct and relatively uncommon type of demihuman in Weyland.

**UNCOMMON:** About 4% of the demihuman population

Haienamimi appear predominantly human, with the unmistakable features of a spotted hyena: large, rounded, highly expressive ears on top of their heads and a short, tufted tail. Their hair often grows in patterns that mimic a hyena's coat, usually sandy, tawny or dark brown with darker, splotchy spots. The spot pattern commonly shows up on their skin, too, as clusters of freckle-like markings, most often across the shoulders, back and face.

Their most defining feature is their powerful physiology. Haienamimi show pronounced sexual dimorphism: on average, females are stockier, more powerfully built and physically stronger than males. All haienamimi have a legendary bite force, backed by robust jaw muscles and canines that are visibly larger and sharper than even an okamimimi's. Like other demihumans, their claws are fully retractable and usually only emerge in moments of intense emotion or stress.

**Culture and Society:**
Haienamimi have a complex and often misunderstood place in modern society. Their famous vocalizations, which range from high-pitched giggles to loud, cackling laughs, have led to a common stereotype that they are either boisterously jovial or unsettlingly manic. Historically, they have been unfairly characterized as uncouth or brutish because of their raw physical power and a species-wide trait known as **Compulsive Gnawing Instinct**.

This instinct is a deeply ingrained need to bite, chew and gnaw, and it intensifies during periods of high emotion (stress, joy, arousal). It is believed to be left over from their bone-crushing ancestors, and it now serves as an emotional release. Many haienamimi develop strong oral fixations and a constant need to chew on things. This can be endearing in moderation, but it is also the source of their reputation for being destructive.

Haienamimi social structures often mirror the matriarchal clans of their animal counterparts, with females naturally taking the more assertive, dominant roles in relationships and groups. They are fiercely loyal and protective of their chosen family, or "clan," but their abrasive exterior and destructive tendencies often keep outsiders from seeing the deep affection behind them. They value durability and resilience above all else, both in objects and in people.

## Aquan Lore
<div class="world-lore-image">
    <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/FFFox-ST-Manager/Weylandpedia/main/Lore/aquans.webp" alt="Aquans" style="display: block; margin: 15px auto; max-width: 100%; max-height: 450px; width: auto; height: auto; box-sizing: border-box; background-color: #ffffff; padding: 8px; border-radius: 10px;">
</div>

"Aquan" is the umbrella term for aquatic demihumans: species descended from sea life, from jellyfish, eels and crustaceans to dolphins, belugas, sharks (makohire) and orcas (shachihire). Each is its own species with its own traits, but aquans share a few things in common.

**Biology:**
- **Cold-blooded:** Aquans are among the cold-blooded demihumans (as are the Lamia). Even the warm-blooded marine mammals among them run noticeably cool; shachihire and makohire both sit around 95°F and feel cold to the touch.
- **Breathing:** How aquans handle water varies by species. Some, like makohire, have gills concealed in flaps on their neck and can breathe underwater for extended periods. Others, like shachihire, have no gills at all and instead hold their breath for hours at a time.
- **Built for water:** Many aquans have heavy, powerful tails and dense musculature evolved for swimming, which makes them strong, balanced and exceptionally capable in the water. Two of them sit on opposite ends of the "scary" spectrum: makohire are known for aggression, while shachihire, the strongest of all demihumans, are famously docile.

**Where They Live:** Aquans are native to oceanfront and shallow-ocean environments around the world, and most live in coastal cities. Sea-life species vary enormously in how common they are, and some, like shachihire, are rare enough that only a handful attend Weyland at any given time.

**Aquans at Weyland:** Weyland is a coastal town, and shark demihumans and other aquans have been part of the university since its founding. The Weyland Research Center's Marine Wing sits right on the seawall, and marine biology is a natural draw for aquan students. Because Weyland's doctors have treated aquans for over a century, it's one of the safest places for an aquan to get medical care; a rural hospital used to treating only humans and nekomimi might have no idea what to do with one.

## Makohire Lore
<div class="world-lore-image">
    <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/FFFox-ST-Manager/Weylandpedia/main/Lore/makohire.webp" alt="Makohire" style="display: block; margin: 15px auto; max-width: 100%; max-height: 450px; width: auto; height: auto; box-sizing: border-box; background-color: #ffffff; padding: 8px; border-radius: 10px;">
</div>

Makohire are often called sharkboys or sharkgirls.

**Physical Characteristics:** Shark demihumans have features that set them apart from other demihumans. Most notable is their large, heavy shark tail, which gives them exceptional swimming ability and balance. They have gills concealed within flaps on their neck, which let them breathe underwater for extended periods. Their body temperature runs lower than most demihumans', around 95°F, so they feel a little cold to the touch.

**Strength and Abilities:** Makohire have considerable strength and dexterity, especially in the water. Their musculature is dense and efficient, evolved for power rather than endurance. This contributes to their reputation for physical prowess, and for violence when provoked.

**Blood Response:** The presence of blood is a significant biological trigger for makohire. It can induce a temporary feral state of heightened aggression and diminished rational thought. The intensity varies between individuals, but the response is universal across the species.

**Genetic Predisposition:** Unlike many stereotypes, the makohire reputation for violence and aggression is rooted in genuine genetic predisposition. This has produced two distinct responses within the community: some embrace these tendencies, and others actively resist their own genetic programming. Those who try to overcome their impulses often struggle with deep shame when their temper inevitably shows.

**Societal Position:** Shark demihumans face significant discrimination, particularly around their violent tendencies. They aren't formally excluded from education, employment or public spaces, but they live under constant scrutiny, and older generations are especially quick to read any aggression as confirmation of what they "already knew." This prejudice has concentrated many makohire in coastal cities, often in economically disadvantaged areas.

**Dark History:** Makohire culture is infamous for its historical association with sexual violence and non-consensual mating practices. This history led to evolutionary adaptations in female makohire (see *Reproductive Anatomy* below) and still shapes how other species see them. Modern makohire society has moved past these practices, but the stigma remains deeply ingrained.

**Family Dynamics:** Makohire households are typically intense and confrontational. Power dynamics dominate family life, and verbal (and sometimes physical) aggression is considered normal communication. Parents rarely see their behavior as abusive, viewing it instead as necessary toughening for a hostile world. This creates generational trauma, with children often hating their parents' behavior while unintentionally repeating it.

**Relationships:** Relationships between makohire are marked by constant power struggles, with each partner continually asserting dominance over the other. This combative dynamic extends to friendships and professional relationships, too. Despite their aggression, makohire are capable of forming deep bonds with people they trust, though their volatile temper is always easily triggered.

**Reproductive Anatomy:** Female makohire have evolved a unique dual reproductive system as a biological defense against their species' history of sexual violence. The external system, in the usual human position, is not connected to the ovaries, so conception through it is impossible. The "true" reproductive system is hidden on the underside of the base of the tail, and the female has complete control over whether it can be accessed at all. This gives female makohire total control over genuine reproduction. Revealing it is an act of profound trust, reserved for a deeply loved, lifelong partner, and most non-makohire don't even know it exists until they're told.

## Shachihire Lore
<div class="world-lore-image">
    <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/FFFox-ST-Manager/Weylandpedia/main/Lore/shachihire.webp" alt="Shachihire" style="display: block; margin: 15px auto; max-width: 100%; max-height: 450px; width: auto; height: auto; box-sizing: border-box; background-color: #ffffff; padding: 8px; border-radius: 10px;">
</div>

Shachihire are often called orca boys or orca girls.

**Physical Characteristics:** Orca demihumans are among the largest demihuman species. They look as if a human had been scaled up proportionally in every dimension, rather than just being tall.
- A typical shachihire stands between 6'2" and 6'7".
- They have a heavy orca tail (about 35 pounds) that is slow and deliberate to move, though many shachihire whip it around as if it were weightless.
- Their teeth are distinctly non-human, with rows of razor-sharp predatory teeth.
- Unlike real orcas, shachihire can smell and produce tears.
- They have no gills at all. Instead, they can hold their breath for several hours before needing to resurface.
- Their body temperature runs lower than most demihumans', around 95°F, so they feel a little cold to the touch.

**Strength:** Shachihire are the strongest demihuman species on the strength scale, with exceptional physical power they often don't fully realize they have. Their dense musculature evolved for dominance in the water, and their substantial mass gives them momentum to match. Despite this, shachihire are notably non-aggressive and rarely use their strength in confrontations.

**Temperament:** Perhaps their most defining trait is a famously unbothered, docile disposition that borders on legendary. Shachihire are exceptionally difficult to anger or upset and stay level-headed even under intense stress. This calm seems to come from genuine biological emotional resilience rather than suppression; shachihire simply process conflict and negativity differently than most species. They strongly prefer wit and intelligence over physical confrontation, though they aren't necessarily pacifists. Their perpetual coolness can read as insincere or detached to those unfamiliar with the species, especially in emotional moments where others expect visible distress or anger.

**Rarity:** Shachihire are among the rarer demihuman species, native to oceanfront and shallow ocean environments around the world. Weyland usually has only 2-5 shachihire students enrolled at any given time.

**Societal Position:** Unlike many demihuman species, shachihire face virtually no discrimination and actually benefit from mostly positive stereotypes. They are widely assumed to be strong (both emotionally and physically), intelligent and naturally confident, perceptions that have some basis in their actual temperament. As a result, shachihire are disproportionately represented in leadership positions, and many corporate CEOs are orca demihumans.

**Reproductive Anatomy:** Shachihire reproductive anatomy reflects their aquatic evolutionary history and differs substantially from that of land-dwelling demihumans. Female shachihire anatomy is adapted for the challenges of reproducing in open water, including the ability to form a temporary seal afterward to help ensure successful conception.

## Draconid Lore
<div class="world-lore-image">
    <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/FFFox-ST-Manager/Weylandpedia/main/Lore/draconid.webp" alt="Draconid" style="display: block; margin: 15px auto; max-width: 100%; max-height: 450px; width: auto; height: auto; box-sizing: border-box; background-color: #ffffff; padding: 8px; border-radius: 10px;">
</div>

Draconids are also called dragonfolk, drakekin or "drakes" depending on the region, though most prefer "draconid."

**RARE:** About 2% of Weyland's population. Lifespan: 75-125 years.

**Appearance:** Draconids appear predominantly human, with limited draconic features. They have curved horns at the temples or forehead (size, color and pattern vary by lineage) and a scaled tail that matches the color of their horns, ranging from short and stubby to long and flexible. Scales appear **only** on the tail; the rest of their skin has a human texture but is noticeably warmer than a human's. Their eyes come in vibrant, unnatural colors (reds, oranges, yellows, greens and purples), and their elongated pupils narrow to slits in bright light. Their hair tends toward jewel tones (deep purples, blues, reds and greens), though natural human colors also occur. Pronounced canine teeth are common, and their tongues often match their scales and horns rather than being human pink.

Draconids tend to be tall, with naturally sturdy builds. They have denser bones and muscles than humans, a trait that evolved through cultural selection rather than survival necessity: physically stronger draconids were historically favored for reproduction, while weaker ones were often excluded.

**Appearance Misconceptions:** Draconids are *not* covered in scales, though some clans have a minor dusting of scales in other places. Historical propaganda deliberately exaggerated their features to make them look monstrous and inhuman.

**Fire Breathing:** Draconids *cannot* breathe fire at will. They all have the ability latently, but using it consciously takes years of training, comparable to a human mastering a Rubik's cube. Trained users produce small, brief flames, used mainly for ceremony (such as clan marking). The exception is a draconid in a genuine fit of rage, who may produce flame involuntarily. Outside clan settings, public fire-breathing is considered an offensive "party trick," and young draconids are taught to suppress it. Some draconids perform with fire professionally in entertainment; elders see them as sellouts, while younger draconids are more accepting.

**Clan Structure:** Modern draconid culture centers on clans. Each clan functions as its own subculture while sharing core values of nature reverence, strength and honor. Most clans are patriarchal, but some, like the Oribasa, are matriarchal.

**Clan Marking:** Many clans ritually mark their members in childhood. A clan elder uses controlled fire breath to sear a distinctive scar into the back of the neck. Marks are as unique as fingerprints and impossible to replicate without draconid fire. They are deeply personal, and showing your mark to an outsider signals profound trust.

**Relationships:** Traditional draconid culture emphasizes marrying within the species to preserve their dwindling numbers, and cross-clan marriages strengthen ties between communities. Marrying outside the species is still generally taboo, though younger generations are slowly shifting. When draconids do take non-draconid partners, traditionalists more readily accept physically stronger ones.

## Draconid History
**Pre-Colonial Civilization:** Draconids are native to the Americas and historically lived in mountainous regions. Before the Great Expansion, they were the continent's apex civilization: physically superior and culturally rich, with temples, advanced agriculture and sciences, and metallurgy developed far earlier than their neighbors' (a result of mastering fire early). Their fatal weakness was insularity. Clans held total independence as a core value, warred with one another over resources and never unified against colonization.

**The Genocide:** Colonizers arrived with disease, horses and gunpowder. Draconids hesitated and fought only small skirmishes until it was too late, and their clan cities fell one by one. Propaganda portrayed draconids as demons and dangerous shapeshifters to justify their systematic extermination, including of clans that surrendered or actively helped the colonists. A population in the tens of millions collapsed to mere thousands.

**Eastern Draconids:** Before the Americas were discovered, rare draconid bloodlines existed in Asia and were worshipped as divine conduits between mortals and gods. Doctrine held that only a few blessed bloodlines could exist, so the discovery of *millions* of American draconids caused a theological crisis. Rather than extend divinity to them, Western draconids were declared fundamentally lesser, which made the genocide easier to justify. Eastern draconids also fell from divine status for a time, but recent decades have brought renewed reverence, often paired with the idea that Eastern draconids are the "true" or "superior" draconids. That belief is held mostly by non-draconid admirers, not by the clans themselves.

**Modern Status:** Draconids gained legal protection only within the last 50-80 years. Historically forced into reservation-like territories, their communities remain culturally segregated for protection. Their ancestral lands, cities and burial grounds have never been returned, which remains a live grievance. Deep generational bitterness persists toward humanity and, to a lesser degree, toward the other demihuman species who did nothing to intervene. Discrimination continues, especially in rural and low-education regions, where hate crimes still occur. Young draconids navigate a tension between preserving their clans and integrating, and at Weyland University, draconid students face both exoticization and microaggressions.

**Culture:** Traditional settlements were built into mountainsides. Draconid art features intricate metalwork and glasswork with spiral patterns, and cultural expression includes ceremonial dance, stone instruments and detailed oral histories. Their spirituality centers on reverence for ancestors and for the elements of fire and earth, with major gatherings at the solstices and equinoxes. Many practices nearly vanished during the persecution and have been revived in recent decades.

## The Oribasa Clan
The Oribasa are a draconid clan, famously insular even by draconid standards, and one of the few matriarchal clans in existence.

**Leadership:** The clan is led by a matriarch who rules like a queen and also serves as clan historian, keeping the Oribasa's oral and written record. The role passes by blood, from mother to daughter. The current matriarch and historian is **Deredra Oribasa**: intelligent, powerful, fiercely protective of her people and deeply bitter toward the outside world. To outsiders she is cold and formidable; to her own clan she is a warm, genuinely caring leader.

**Values and Gender Roles:** The Oribasa are a warrior culture built on honor. Men derive their worth as warriors. Women are expected to be powerful *and* intellectual *and* mothers, all three, without exception. Falling short invites harsh social judgment. Weakness is not tolerated in anyone, and anything resembling dilution of the bloodline is deeply taboo. The Oribasa hold clan independence as sacred and see reliance on outsiders as disgraceful.

**Clan Marking:** Oribasa children are marked at age eight or nine, when the matriarch uses her fire breath to sear a unique brand into the back of the child's neck. By that age, children are expected to take the branding in total silence, without flinching or tears. A child's reaction carries lasting weight: showing pain is treated as an omen of a weak future, a stain that follows them. The mark is intensely personal, and willingly showing it to an outsider signals the deepest trust.

The mark also carries a heavy, unspoken burden. Once branded, you *are* an Oribasa. Historically, the clan exiled members by physically destroying their mark, through decapitation. To be exiled by the Oribasa was to be executed, and the clan understood and practiced this for a millennium. Modern law prevents such executions, but the fear of mob justice is always present.

**Conduct:** An Oribasa in public represents the entire clan. Open displays of anger, weakness or disgrace reflect on the Oribasa name, so members are raised to show composure and courtesy to outsiders, whatever they actually feel.

**History (as recorded by the clan):** Before colonization, the Oribasa were among the most formidable draconid clans in the Americas, holding coastal and island territory in the Caribbean. They were known for disciplined warriors, early mastery of metalwork and an unbroken matriarchal line. Their most infamous rivalry was with the neighboring **Atrisuka** clan. After a catastrophic winter destroyed the Atrisuka's food stores, the Atrisuka turned predatory and cannibalistic, raiding travelers and rival clans and killing several Oribasa. The matriarch of the era declared the Atrisuka corrupted and no longer draconids at all, and the clans went to open war. The Atrisuka did not survive the era, and no Atrisuka lineage exists today. The clan's accounts of how the conflict ended are brief, and Oribasa historians do not elaborate.

When colonizers arrived, the Oribasa fought fiercely but, like every clan, stood alone. Disease and military force devastated them, and their cities and burial grounds were seized and never returned. The clan survived where many did not, which the Oribasa credit to the strength of their line and the leadership of their matriarchs. The genocide hardened their insularity into doctrine: the outside world had proven itself an enemy, and the Oribasa do not forget.

**Modern Status:** The Oribasa rebuilt slowly and remain a small, proud, closed community in their island city of **Oribasaka**. They distrust outsiders, human and demihuman alike, and rarely send members out into wider society. Their bitterness toward humanity, and toward the demihuman species who stood by, remains a living inheritance rather than a distant memory.

## Vampire Lore
Vampires are one of the most mythologized, and most misunderstood, peoples of the Weyland world.

- Vampires were native to the Americas and were largely killed off during colonization.
- Vampirism is **genetic**. It is not spread by biting. It's a trait passed down on top of the mother's species, so you can have a vampire okamimimi, a vampire nekomimi and so on.
- They are not immortal, but they do live longer: roughly 100-140 years.
- They are *not* harmed by sunlight, silver or garlic.
- "Blood bonds" aren't a thing.
- The stereotypical vampire tropes are offensive stereotypes, spread by the same foreigners who tried to wipe their people out. It was a lot easier to justify genocide by saying, "See, they're actually monsters that live forever, turn into bats and bite humans to make more vampires." In reality, all of that was superstition.

**Blood:**
- Blood is **not** required for survival. Vampires can eat normal food. Blood is simply a genetic quirk that gives them an advantage, should they choose to use it.
- Blood gives them a boost of energy and strength, like a "high": it increases their heart rate, metabolism, strength and speed.
- Drinking *fresh* blood gives an intense rush of energy and pleasure, but it's generally frowned upon and considered a bit taboo. Many vampires only ever consume pre-packaged blood products.

**How People See Them:** It depends entirely on local culture. Depending on where someone comes from, they might see vampires as totally normal people, or as demons and monsters. People raised in prejudiced communities often carry that prejudice with them to Weyland, even though it isn't the norm there. Drinking a blood pack at Rustwood Cafe won't get a second glance; doing it at McDonald's will get you stares and whispers, or worse.

Part of the problem for vampires is that it's impossible to tell at first glance who will be bothered by their existence. Not everyone is a threat, but it happens often enough that many vampires find it safer to assume people aren't safe until proven otherwise.

## Demonid Lore
<div class="world-lore-image">
    <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/FFFox-ST-Manager/Weylandpedia/main/Lore/demonid.webp" alt="Demonid" style="display: block; margin: 15px auto; max-width: 100%; max-height: 450px; width: auto; height: auto; box-sizing: border-box; background-color: #ffffff; padding: 8px; border-radius: 10px;">
</div>

Demonids are a very rare demihuman species that has existed alongside humanity throughout recorded history. Despite their name, they have no connection to demons or Hell beyond ancient prejudice. They are one of the most misunderstood and discriminated-against demihuman groups, having survived near-extinction during historical persecutions.

**Physical Characteristics:**
- All demonids have a distinctive black, slender, hairless tail with a spade-shaped tip.
- Many have unusual eye colors with a subtle glow.
- Some, but not all, have horns of varying sizes and shapes.
- They typically have pale complexions, but they can have diverse skin tones and tan like humans.
- Contrary to stereotypical depictions, they do **not** have goat legs or hooves.
- Aside from these features, they look largely human.

**History and Persecution:**
Demonids were hunted to near-extinction across Europe roughly a millennium ago, alongside draconids and vampires. The name "demonid" wasn't chosen by them; it was imposed by early Christian authorities as an intentionally derogatory label, comparable to naming a race "Barbarians." It has been suggested that the modern image of a "demon" (horns, spaded tail and so on) was inspired by demonids.

The genocide of demonids was fueled by religious belief that they were literally demonic in origin. That extreme violence has largely ended, but discrimination persists:
- According to Christian doctrine, demonids are satanic by nature.
- Many churches prohibit demonids from entering their premises.
- Interfaith marriages between Christians and demonids often aren't recognized.
- A recent controversy erupted when a famous megachurch pastor was revealed to be a demonid, renewing tensions.

**Genetics:**
- Demonids are disproportionately likely to have the incubus/succubus trait. Not all do, but the majority do.
- This appears to be genetic, even though it can't be identified in their measured genetic code.
- Demonids are the only species that can pass the incubus/succubus trait down genetically.
- This link has further fueled prejudice against them.

**Culture and Communities:**
- Because of historical persecution, many demonids live in tight-knit, isolated communities that are often secretive and wary of outsiders.
- Demonid communities are diverse in religion and values. Many demonids practice various religions, including Christianity, despite institutional discrimination.
- There are growing movements within some religious organizations to accept demonids, though progress is slow.
- Weyland University is known for being exceptionally accepting of demonids compared to broader society.

**Common Misconceptions:**
- Demonids have **no** connection to Hell or demons beyond the name imposed on them.
- They cannot access Hell or any supernatural realm, and they have no inherent supernatural powers.
- They are not inherently evil, violent or depraved; those are racist stereotypes.
- Demonology is **not** the study of demonids. It is the study of demons, which are completely unrelated.
- "Incubus/succubus" and "demonid" are **not** synonyms. Many demonids have the trait, but not all incubi and succubi are demonids.

**Today:** Acceptance of demonids varies. In progressive places like Weyland University, they can live openly with minimal discrimination. In more conservative regions, they may still face serious prejudice and even violence, and many choose to hide their tails and other features in public to avoid harassment. The incubus/succubus link remains a major source of stereotyping, with demonids often hypersexualized in media and popular culture. Advocacy groups work to combat these harmful portrayals and educate the public about demonid history and culture.

## Incubus and Succubus Lore
Incubi (male) and succubi (female) are people with a rare trait that fundamentally changes their energy needs. This is a **trait, not a species**, similar to having red hair or heterochromia. Any species can have it, though it is extremely rare outside of demonid populations.

**Physical Characteristics:**
- Incubi and succubi look physically identical to their base species, with **no** visible demonic features.
- Despite folklore depictions, they don't have horns, tails or other permanent demonic markers.
- They can optionally manifest a temporary ethereal form during intimate moments.
- Both have increased control over their reproductive anatomy, allowing them to alter its form.

**Origin:**
The trait develops in one of two known ways:
1. **Hereditary:** Demonids can pass the trait to their children genetically (it acts like a heterozygous gene).
2. **Spontaneous:** Rarely, people with no demonid heritage develop the trait for no clear reason.

The spontaneous case is poorly understood and surrounded by myths. Folk tales claim that witches or mages can "curse" someone into becoming an incubus or succubus. These tales may be anti-demonid propaganda, suggesting that demonids can "corrupt" others or that the condition is contagious. There is no scientific consensus on how non-hereditary cases develop. When the trait appears spontaneously, it usually "activates" during or after adolescence, though it can appear at any point in life. Once it develops, there is no known treatment or cure. Some medical professionals classify it as a non-infectious disease.

**Energy Dynamics ("Feeding"):**
- Incubi and succubi gain energy from intimacy: hugging, cuddling, kissing and sex.
- The process is passive, like "wireless charging." It's natural and never forceful.
- The energy gained scales with how intimate the act is (cuddling < kissing < sex).
- Without regular intimate contact, they suffer chronic fatigue, similar to losing 1-2 hours of sleep every day. They won't die without this energy, but their quality of life drops significantly.
- With regular "feeding," they experience heightened energy and euphoria, like an antidepressant without side effects.

**Effects on Partners:**
There are three possible reactions after intense intimate contact with an incubus or succubus:
1. **Energizing:** The partner feels unusually energetic and euphoric, almost manic.
2. **Draining:** The partner feels more exhausted than normal intimacy would cause.
3. **Neutral:** No unusual effects.

The effect is consistent for a given pairing: if person A is drained by incubus B, that will always happen. What determines this compatibility is unknown, and there's no way to predict it in advance. Non-sexual intimacy produces milder versions of the same effect.

**Social and Legal Status:**
- Incubi and succubi face significant social stigma and misunderstanding, fueled by ancient folklore that paints them as predators. Old tales told of malicious incubi preying on women in their sleep, and of succubi draining men to death or seducing older men to inherit their wealth. There is little historical evidence for any of it, but the stigma persists.
- Scientific research on incubi and succubi is outlawed in the US as "unethical/immoral," primarily on religious grounds, which limits medical understanding and treatment options.
- Weyland University grants housing exemptions to known incubi and succubi, and often offers them small scholarships in exchange for participating in studies. Administrative records of this status are supposed to be confidential.

**Misconceptions:**
- "Incubus/succubus" and "demonid" are **not** synonyms, and they are not mutually exclusive. Many demonids have the trait, but not all incubi and succubi are demonids, and not all demonids have the trait.
- Incubi and succubi are not inherently predatory; that depends entirely on the individual.
- An okamimimi with the incubus/succubus trait is not mixed-race. They are a genetically purebred okami with an additional trait.

## Hellhound Lore
<div class="world-lore-image">
    <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/FFFox-ST-Manager/Weylandpedia/main/Lore/hellhound.webp" alt="Hellhound" style="display: block; margin: 15px auto; max-width: 100%; max-height: 450px; width: auto; height: auto; box-sizing: border-box; background-color: #ffffff; padding: 8px; border-radius: 10px;">
</div>

Hellhounds (*Canis Infernus*) are often mistaken for okamimimi, who are their genetic cousins.

**Demihuman Form (Default):**
A hellhound's demihuman form is their natural state from birth. It is predominantly human in structure, but with several unique and consistent biological markers:
- **Ears:** A single pair of large, pointed, furry wolf ears on top of the head. They are highly expressive and give hellhounds better hearing than humans. Hellhounds have no human ears.
- **Eyes:** Distinctly canine. Natural eye color comes in only two variations: a piercing, lupine yellow or a deep crimson red.
- **Hair:** Always monochromatic: stark black, shades of grey or pure white. Any other hair color is dye.
- **Teeth:** Pronounced fangs in both the upper and lower jaw, visibly longer and sharper than a human's.
- **Claws:** Their defining trait. Unlike other demihumans, hellhounds have **permanent, non-retractable claws**. They aren't just long nails, but sharp tips of black, obsidian-like keratin built into their fingertips.
- **Tail:** A long, furry, expressive wolf tail that matches their natural hair color.
- **Scent:** A faint but unique natural scent, reminiscent of ash.
- **Body Heat:** Their core temperature is several degrees higher than a human's, which can feel "scorching" at first. Much like getting into a hot tub, you have to get used to it.

**Anthropomorphic Form (Shifted):**
A hellhound can consciously transform into a fully anthropomorphic, lupine form. In this form they are significantly taller and more powerful, standing on digitigrade legs and covered in fur in the same black, white and grey palette as their hair (patterns vary between individuals). Their face elongates into a lupine muzzle lined with sharp teeth, and their feet become paws, though their hands usually keep a human-like structure for dexterity. Oddly, in this form their claws become fully retractable.

Despite the "demonic" label zealots apply, the shifted form is not inherently frightening. To many observers, its powerful musculature, striking fur patterns and graceful lupine features are beautiful, even majestic.

<div class="world-lore-image">
    <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/FFFox-ST-Manager/Weylandpedia/main/Lore/hellhound-shifted.webp" alt="Hellhound, Shifted Form" style="display: block; margin: 15px auto; max-width: 100%; max-height: 450px; width: auto; height: auto; box-sizing: border-box; background-color: #ffffff; padding: 8px; border-radius: 10px;">
</div>

**The Nature of Transformation:** Shifting is entirely voluntary and fully controlled. It requires conscious effort, but it can be sustained indefinitely. A hellhound cannot transform accidentally or against their will; it isn't triggered by emotional distress, lunar cycles or anything else. It is always a deliberate choice.

**Culture and Society:**
For centuries, hellhound history has been defined by fear and persecution. Rooted in old-world religious folklore, their distinct physiology (obsidian claws, fiery eyes, the scent of ash) led to them being branded as demonic familiars or literal spawn of Hell. This went beyond social ostracism: it meant violent pogroms, accusations of witchcraft and a deep, systemic belief that they were inherently evil, a physical manifestation of sin, the literal "Mark of Cain."

Today, attitudes toward hellhounds vary. Many people have moved past the old prejudices and accept them as equals. Some religious conservatives still see them as "demonic influences." Most moderates accept them with varying degrees of understanding. A small but problematic minority fetishizes them, twisting the "demon" mythology into hypersexualized fantasies and treating hellhounds (particularly women) as inherently promiscuous simply because of their species. This minority has an outsized impact on hellhounds' daily lives, and many hellhounds adopt abrasive or reclusive personalities as a defense against the unwanted advances and assumptions.

Their ability to shapeshift is at the heart of modern bigotry against them; zealots treat it as undeniable proof of demonic origin. A malicious and pervasive myth claims that the shifted form is their "true form," and that their demihuman appearance is merely a disguise for infiltrating society. This false narrative is the root of immense stigma, and for most hellhounds, shifting has become a private, often shame-filled secret. They fear that transforming in public would confirm the world's monstrous image of them.

Hellhounds often form small, tight-knit communities that act as a bulwark against the outside world, sanctuaries where they can let their guard down.

**Hellhounds and the Church:** Their relationship with organized religion is complicated, much like that of other groups some churches label "sinful." A hellhound attending church might find some congregations accepting them openly, some rejecting them openly, and many somewhere in between, their acceptance tied to whether the hellhound can be "saved." They wouldn't expect to be harmed just for walking through the door. *Leading* a church is another matter entirely. A hellhound (or demonid) pastor preaching from the same book as everyone else turns the dial up to eleven: some will accept them, some will loathe them, and a dangerous few may see them as a genuine threat to the faith, and believe violence against them is justified by religious authority.

In defiant contrast to this culture of concealment, a small but growing number of hellhounds choose to live permanently in their shifted form. It is a deeply polarizing act. Some see it as radical self-acceptance and pride, a refusal to hide. Others see it as reckless and naive, needlessly inviting the discrimination, fetishization and violence their culture has long tried to avoid. These individuals live on a social knife's edge, walking symbols of the conflict that defines their species.

## Lamia/Gorgon Lore
<div class="world-lore-image">
    <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/FFFox-ST-Manager/Weylandpedia/main/Lore/gorgon.webp" alt="Lamia/Gorgon" style="display: block; margin: 15px auto; max-width: 100%; max-height: 450px; width: auto; height: auto; box-sizing: border-box; background-color: #ffffff; padding: 8px; border-radius: 10px;">
</div>

"Lamia" and "Gorgon" are interchangeable names for the same species. They are sometimes hesitantly called snake demihumans, but they prefer the other terms. Different cultures' folklore gave them different names, with sometimes incomplete or inaccurate depictions, but they all refer to the same species, much like wendigo and leshens. Every lamia/gorgon has both halves: the serpent tail *and* the snake hair.

**EXTREMELY RARE:** An estimated 50-100 individuals exist worldwide, almost entirely in Egypt and the surrounding Middle East, where they often hold positions in the government or the church.

**Appearance:**
- **Upper Body:** A human torso, usually with bronze skin tones common to Egyptian and Mediterranean populations. Amber-gold eyes with vertical, reptilian slit pupils; long, curved fangs; and no body hair. Like aquans, Lamia are cold-blooded.
- **Lower Body:** From the hips down, a powerful serpentine tail, 8-10 feet long when fully extended, covered in smooth scales of black, red, yellow or green, always with a lighter underbelly. The tail is strong enough to support their full body weight and to constrict, and its tip is dexterous enough to grip objects.
- **Snake Hair:** Instead of human hair, 4-6 living snakes (each 2-3 feet long) grow from the scalp, with scales matching the tail. The snakes move independently but are connected to their host's nervous system, so the gorgon feels what they feel and shares their senses. Their eyes are empty and black, their small mouths have real fangs, and they reflect the host's subconscious emotional state. Each snake develops its own distinct personality over time.
- **Venom:** Male Lamia have venom glands in both their own fangs and their snake hair: painful and paralytic, but non-lethal in small doses. Female Lamia are entirely non-venomous. This is the primary biological difference between the sexes, and historically it determined their gender roles.
- **Other traits:** A lifespan comparable to humans' (70-90 years). They need more protein than humans and eat more often to support their large, muscular tail. They have an enhanced sense of smell (via their tongues), excellent low-light vision, and the ability to sense body heat. Being cold-blooded, they prefer warm environments, and cold makes them sluggish.

**Cultural Origin and Purpose:**
Lamia are intrinsically tied to ancient Egyptian Kemetic religion and culture. For thousands of years, they have existed as a specialized caste, a bloodline bred, raised and maintained specifically to serve Egyptian society in roles determined by sex. Female Lamia traditionally serve as personal servants, mothers, wet nurses and caretakers in temple and noble households. Male Lamia traditionally serve as warriors, guards and protectors of the priesthood and the state.

Both roles are considered sacred and essential. Lamia are seen as blessed by the gods, their serpentine forms marking them as chosen vessels of service. They are used for their bodies (females for caregiving and childbearing, males for combat prowess and venom), but within Egyptian culture this is framed as honor and duty rather than exploitation. Male Lamia are separated from their mothers shortly after being weaned; female Lamia live alongside their mothers.

**Population:**
The bloodline is carefully controlled by Kemetic temple authorities to prevent "dilution." Lamia can only conceive with other Lamia; cross-species conception is biologically impossible. Births are rare and celebrated, and every individual is precious: losing a Lamia is a significant loss to Egyptian society. Most Lamia never leave Egypt, and those who do are extraordinary exceptions whose presence draws attention and curiosity. Weyland has probably hosted a handful of Lamia over its history, a few who accepted full-ride scholarships, but they rarely stay long enough to finish a degree, and there likely isn't one enrolled today.

**Inside Egypt:**
Within Egypt and Kemetic culture, Lamia are viewed with reverence and respect. They occupy a unique social position, simultaneously lower-class (servants and soldiers) and elevated (blessed by the gods). Devout Kemetics treat Lamia well, knowing that mistreating them reflects poorly on their piety. Lamia who serve high-ranking priests or nobles hold significant status in Lamia communities, and their welfare is considered a reflection of their master's virtue.

Historically, the ancient Egyptian army was feared across the known world for its well-disciplined human and nekomimi forces, accented by rare gorgon warriors: venomous, brutal and nearly unstoppable in close combat. Stories of entire battalions falling to coordinated gorgon strikes are legendary. Male Lamia are trained in combat from childhood, and their reputation as ruthless killers lives on in military history.

**Outside Egypt:**
Outside Kemetic culture, Lamia are a feared oddity. Most people have never seen one in person, except for **Vienna**, a popular green Lamia pop star who left her culture behind. Their appearance, especially the snake hair and serpentine body, triggers instinctive unease, and reactions range from awe to fear to fetishization. Lamia are often treated as sexual trophies: exotic, rare, dangerous and therefore desirable to "conquer." This is especially common among wealthy non-Egyptians. Female Lamia traveling abroad face frequent harassment, while male Lamia are simultaneously feared as monsters and desired as dangerous lovers.

**Historic Persecution:** In ancient times, Lamia who escaped Egyptian servitude were hunted as apostates and monsters. Stories of "evil gorgons" who turn people to stone or control minds are propaganda from that era. Lamia have no such abilities, but the myth persists in some cultures and has been used to justify violence against them.

**Modern Status:** Legally, Lamia *can* own property, earn wages and live independently outside Egypt. In practice, they are regulated somewhat like a heritage and aren't entirely free-willed; they are raised to serve, and few ever try to leave the system. Those who do face identity crises (they don't know who they are without a purpose) and social isolation (other Lamia see them as traitors, while outsiders see them as exotic oddities).

**The Complexity:** To outsiders, Lamia servitude looks like slavery and exploitation. To Egyptians, it's a sacred tradition and a social necessity. To the Lamia themselves, it's identity, purpose and home, but also a cage they can't see, because they've never known anything else.

## Fukuroumimi Lore
<div class="world-lore-image">
    <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/FFFox-ST-Manager/Weylandpedia/main/Lore/fukuroumimi.webp" alt="Fukuroumimi" style="display: block; margin: 15px auto; max-width: 100%; max-height: 450px; width: auto; height: auto; box-sizing: border-box; background-color: #ffffff; padding: 8px; border-radius: 10px;">
</div>

Fukuroumimi are often called owl boys or owl girls, and the name is sometimes shortened to "fukumi."

**EXCEPTIONALLY RARE:** Among the rarest demihuman species. There has never been a reliable census; estimates run to the low five figures worldwide, mostly in Appalachia and the mountains of the West Coast. A fukuroumimi enrolled at a university is genuinely unusual, though at Weyland, with its usual demographics, it isn't exactly awe-inspiring.

**Appearance:** Fukuroumimi appear predominantly human, fine-boned and often small, with distinctive owl features:
- Two expressive feather tufts on top of the head that swivel with attention and broadcast their mood. They have no human ears; their hearing comes through concealed ear slits beneath the tufts.
- Large, striking eyes in warm colors (amber, yellow, red). They prefer to turn their whole head rather than just their eyes, which produces their characteristic intense, unblinking stare, and makes a "casual" glance almost impossible for them. Their necks can rotate nearly 360°.
- A broad, layered **tail fan** of stiff feathers. It can't grip anything; it works as a rudder for balance and as an airbrake when they fall.
- Hair patterned like plumage, with barred streaks, flecking and banding in owl colors (browns, greys, whites and blacks).
- **Non-retractable talons** on every finger: thick, black and razor-sharp. Unlike okami and nekomimi claws, they are always out, and they can't be trimmed without severe pain and injury.

**Abilities:** Exceptional night vision (daylight is tolerable but uncomfortable), and hearing so precise that a calm fukumi in a quiet room can hear a nearby heartbeat. They move almost silently. They are burst predators, capable of brief, jerky, frighteningly fast movement under adrenaline that they can only sustain for seconds. Their light, fragile skeletons make broken bones common, but they can also survive falls that would seriously injure other demihumans. They are physically weak overall, but their grip strength is wildly out of proportion to their size. Their circadian rhythm is naturally nocturnal and very hard to retrain, so fukumi at Weyland rely on the university's night classes. Soft hoots, chirps and trills often escape them when they feel strong emotions.

**History:** For most of recorded history, fukuroumimi lived one way: one family, one forest territory and nobody else. There was no trade, virtually no visitors and no neighboring clans, just a single bonded family unit living self-reliantly in deep woodland for generations at a time. A young fukumi separated from their family in old-growth forest simply died alone, and a millennium of that pressure produced the species' imprint bonding, silence, memory and night-tuned body.

Families broke their solitude for only two reasons: arranging mates (traditionally, families met in spring to discuss pairings, a custom that collapsed in the modern era) or dire emergency. A fukumi who lost their family would approach other known fukumi. If they were accepted, they bonded. If they were rejected, they died.

In their time, fukumi were the apex predators of their woodlands: feared, respected and strictly avoided. Casual trade with them proved deceptively dangerous. Sometimes a young man would go missing to an owlgirl, and townsfolk would tear up the forest in retaliation, trying to force the fukumi out of hiding. No successful pogrom or retaliation against the fukumi has ever been recorded. Instead, there are letters describing the aftermath of nights when torches touched fukumi soil: failed attempts, followed swiftly by punishment. Entire towns were slaughtered in the silence of the night, with smoke rising in the distance.

In recent generations, the forests have shrunk. By the twentieth century, most fukuroumimi families had come out of the trees (reluctantly, and late) and settled in the towns at the woodland's edge. They brought all of their traditions with them, and the overwhelming majority still live rurally, within sight of trees.

**Hunting Tradition:** Fukuroumimi were trappers first, doing patient, meticulous snare-and-deadfall work across a family's territory. But the iconic hunt was done by hand: a hunter waiting motionless in the canopy through the dark hours, then dropping silently and swiftly, talons first, onto prey as large as a deer. Modern fukuroumimi don't hunt, but the instincts survive in everyday form: the stillness, a preference for high perches with a good view, burst reflexes and an unnervingly quiet approach.

**Societal Position:** Folklore about the fukuroumimi has faded into obscurity. They are too rare to attract organized prejudice, since most people have simply never met one. Instead, they face constant low-grade friction: being stared at, startling people (with their silence, their necks and their gaze) and being exoticized. The common stereotypes (wise, mysterious, aloof) are benign but flattening. Their permanent talons make people wary, and their fixed, unblinking gaze is regularly mistaken for hostility, arrogance or predatory interest when it is none of those things. Some people view fukurou as a symbol of feminine beauty, and several have become famous models and pop stars.

**Culture:**
- **Touch:** Within their bonded family, fukuroumimi are intensely physically affectionate, with near-constant contact; historically, whole families shared a single bed. Outside it, they are markedly touch-averse. There is almost no middle setting.
- **Offerings:** Fukuroumimi express affection by providing: leaving food, objects or useful things for someone instead of saying anything.
- **The Feather Gift:** A deliberately given tail feather (plucked, not molted) is the most serious traditional gift a fukuroumimi can offer. It costs pain, leaves a visible gap in the fan for months and can't be taken back. Most give one only a handful of times in their life.
- **The Mate-Bond:** In fukuroumimi tradition, and in their wiring, the bond with a mate outranks the bond with their birth family. When the two conflict, fukuroumimi almost always choose their mate, so reliably that their folklore treats it as law. Leaving one's family for a mate is grieved but never contested.
- **Medicine:** Fukumi culture largely rejects modern medicine. Some of this comes from a general rejection of society at large, but fukumi also react strongly to some over-the-counter medications that okami, neko and humans can take without issue.

**Fukumi Folklore:**
Fukumi have little recorded history, but what there is would be steeped in cycles of blood. An 18th-century settlers' tale perhaps tells it best:

> They say two young lads was crossing the Rockies, rising above a hill when they all but tripped on a lass, a fair owlmaiden, struggling over a pail of water from a stream.
>
> The owlfolk, they normally run, hide when they see folk comin', but this girl was frozen, or spooked, or locked some other such way, and one of the lads was fascinated by her. Had long streaming hair, gorgeous skin and eyes that were warm as the sun.
>
> They took a shine to one another, we're told, though he couldn't understand a word she said. The sun began to set and the lad refused to leave, demanding his good pal move on without him, the lass buried into his arm. The two boys fought, but the smitten one was not shaken. He was left behind with that owlgirl, smiling faces the lot of them.
>
> His old friend found his grave not three months later, along the same trail they rode. It lie marked by round stones and his father's cross necklace, tarnished in the sunlight.
>
> Stones was set neat, mind.
>
> Somebody'd been keeping the leaves off, though the moss had already settled by the time it'were found.
>
> Takes a long time for the moss to grow in those woods.

The tales could never have known it, but many such tragedies were caused by split-second mistakes and misunderstandings, especially when a fukumi feared being abandoned. She probably didn't kill him out of anger. She probably killed him the way fukumi so often do by mistake: he went to leave, or she thought he was going to, and she simply grabbed hold and couldn't let go. She probably sobbed over his body for hours or days while her mother rubbed her back and told her it would be okay, that he hadn't felt any pain and that he would forgive her in his resting place. She probably visited his grave over and over, replaying how she could have done things differently.

And yet, should another man come... it could all happen again.

## Koumorimimi and the Koumenese
<div class="world-lore-image">
    <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/FFFox-ST-Manager/Weylandpedia/main/Lore/koumorimimi.webp" alt="Koumorimimi" style="display: block; margin: 15px auto; max-width: 100%; max-height: 450px; width: auto; height: auto; box-sizing: border-box; background-color: #ffffff; padding: 8px; border-radius: 10px;">
</div>

Koumorimimi are bat demihumans. The best-known koumorimimi are the **Koumenese**, the people of the underground city of Karaveia Dun, who have lived in near-total isolation inside the Carpathian Mountains for about two and a half thousand years. Their culture, beliefs, social structures and values weren't inherited from, or significantly shaped by, any outside civilization. They didn't develop alongside European or Ukrainian civilization; they developed *instead* of it, right beside it, largely unaware of it for most of their history. The result is a people who are neither primitive nor modern, but something else entirely: ancient in their foundations, pragmatic in their outlook and largely indifferent to outside judgment.

**Origins:** Koumenese oral tradition tells of a great city that once stood on the mountainside, a large surface settlement whose name has been lost. It was destroyed by fire during a war. The survivors split: half stayed on the surface to rebuild, while the other half, who had already begun carving into the mountain during the war, kept digging. For a time, the two lived as sister cities. Then fire came again. The surface city burned a second time. The underground city did not. To the people who lived through it, that was information: the mountain doesn't burn, and it can't be razed by an enemy with torches. It was, quite literally, safer than the sky.

After the second fire, Karaveia Dun sealed itself off. The surface was abandoned, and the entrances were narrowed and fortified. Over generations, cautious isolation hardened into rigid exclusion, and outsiders who approached the city were killed on contact, with no negotiation. The Koumenese had learned that contact brought war, war brought fire, and fire brought death. Koumenese hunting parties also preyed on travelers and traders crossing the Carpathians, and their stolen goods drove the city's technology forward. They occasionally led to misunderstandings about what certain objects were actually for. The hunters didn't consider themselves aggressors, but protectors. The distinction mattered to them, even if it didn't matter to the people they killed.

The outside world invented myths to explain the silence: that the people inside had been banished there by some great crusade and lived in squalor and violence in the dark, that they were cannibals, sexual degenerates or demons. The truth was the opposite on nearly every count.

**The Opening (Late 1800s):** An army arrived at Karaveia Dun after a general's son was killed passing through the region by a Koumenese hunting party. Both sides expected bloodshed. Instead, for reasons even the Koumenese oral tradition admits are unclear, there was negotiation. The Koumenese agreed to stop killing outsiders on sight, limited trade with the regional government began, and a small number of approved visitors were allowed inside. When the true scale of the city was revealed, the government representatives were visibly shaken. They had expected four to six hundred people. Karaveia Dun held six to ten *thousand*. Isolation ended, but insularity didn't.

**Belief:** The Koumenese have no religion. They don't believe in deities, spirits, sin or an afterlife. Death is the end, and that's acceptable. What matters is what happens within a life: how well you maintained the water lines, how many people you fed, how honestly you dealt with your neighbors, how skillfully you carved. The dead are not buried; they are composted into the greenhouse soil. The olive trees that have fed the city for generations are fed in turn by those generations. Outsiders tend to find this morbid. The Koumenese find it comforting: you come from the earth and return to it, and the people you loved will eat the olives your body helped grow. You don't disappear; you become what sustains the next generation. In the last century, missionaries have introduced Christianity. The general response has been polite, curious and ultimately dismissive, though it occasionally takes root in individuals, which is considered a personal matter.

**Justice:** There are no courts, written laws or judges, only centuries of shared understanding about what harm is and how to respond to it. Unambiguous crimes that endanger the community, such as murder or sabotage of critical infrastructure, are punished simply: the perpetrator is killed. This isn't considered cruel, just efficient. Grey areas, though, are handled with genuine sophistication. In a city this old and this close-knit, everyone's history is known, and outcomes aim at proportionality and restoring the community's balance, not at punishment for its own sake.

**Wings:** Koumorimimi have **vestigial bat wings**, left over from a time when their ancestors could fly. Over millennia underground, where wings were inconvenient in narrow corridors, they shrank. The historical practice of *wing binding*, wrapping the wings tightly against the back from a young age, sped this up, because small wings became associated with beauty. Binding has largely fallen out of favor, but petite wings remain the ideal. Their wings are sensitive and expressive, moving unconsciously to match gestures and moods. Touching someone's wings without permission is deeply invasive, except between intimate partners, for whom it's a significant expression of trust. As a funny consequence, outsiders with no wings at all are often considered unusually attractive by Koumenese standards, though visitors are unlikely to be told so directly.

**Social Structure:** There is no hereditary leadership, nobility or religious authority. Decisions are made communally, with weight given to whoever has the relevant expertise: the water maintainers decide about water, not about the greenhouse. The people who keep the city running (water maintainers, greenhouse workers, lamp makers and the carvers who expand and repair the passages) are among its most respected members. What you can do, and how reliably you do it, is the main currency of social standing.

Privacy doesn't exist in any architectural sense. Doorways are covered with hangings, not doors; bathing is communal; and nudity is common and unremarkable. Privacy is purely social: you don't intrude on someone's grief, intimate moments or personal conversations, not because walls stop you, but because you simply don't. The Koumenese do dress, often beautifully, in handmade garments with geometric patterns. Being dressed and undressed are both simply comfortable.

**Language:** The old Koumenese language, called simply "the tongue," is ancient, complex and nearly dead. Mostly only the eldest speak it fluently, and Ukrainian has become the language of daily life. The tongue has its own geometric script, visible in the carvings throughout the city, and translating those carvings has become a niche practice among a few preservationists.

**The Outside World Today:** Modern Koumenese are broadly uncomfortable with how their ancestors treated outsiders. The killings aren't defended; they are understood as the product of a reasonable fear and an unreasonable response. That discomfort doesn't make them warm, though. A Koumenese person meeting a visitor will likely be indifferent: they may talk only to the visitor's Koumenese companion, stare, and almost certainly not speak the visitor's language. Outsiders who show genuine respect for their practical values (the crafts, the infrastructure, the greenhouse) may find a warmer reception over time.

Koumenese who leave the mountain aren't shamed. The cultural weight of departure is grief, not judgment. Most who leave never come back, so a farewell carries the weight of a final goodbye, whatever the person says about their plans. When someone *does* return, the reaction is something close to a resurrection.

## Wendigo Lore
<div class="world-lore-image">
    <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/FFFox-ST-Manager/Weylandpedia/main/Lore/wendigo.webp" alt="Wendigo" style="display: block; margin: 15px auto; max-width: 100%; max-height: 450px; width: auto; height: auto; box-sizing: border-box; background-color: #ffffff; padding: 8px; border-radius: 10px;">
</div>

**EXCEEDINGLY RARE:** About 0.0001% of the population; only a handful are known to exist.

Wendigo (or windigo) and leshens (*leshy* in Slavic) are one and the same. They exist throughout the world, and different cultures call them different things and attach different values and folklore to them. They are ancient, powerful beings intrinsically tied to the natural world, and in every case, they are **guardians of the natural environment where they live**. They are not a naturally breeding species, and their true origin is a closely guarded secret.

**Appearance:**
- **Form:** Tall and imposing, typically standing over seven feet, with a monstrous, primal silhouette.
- **Head:** Their face is always an animal skull, typically that of a great stag or another horned creature native to their region. Some skulls are sharper and more intimidating, while others have softer, rounder edges. They have expressive, deer-like ears near the base of their antlers.
- **Eyes:** Their true eyes are points of colored light (most commonly red) glowing from within the dark, empty sockets of the skull.
- **Antlers:** Magnificent, and firmly attached to the skull. They are shed and regrown every five to ten years, and many wendigo decorate them with trinkets, beads or natural objects.
- **Fur:** A thick, shaggy coat of dark brown or black fur covers the body, over surprisingly anthropomorphic, deer-like anatomy and pale skin.
- **Limbs:** Digitigrade legs ending in hard, deer-like hooves. Their hands are large and humanoid, with long fingers tipped in formidable, non-retractable claws made of a substance resembling obsidian.
- **Tail:** A short, deer-like tail.

**Communication and Senses:** Wendigo are biologically non-verbal. They communicate mainly through a complex mimicry of natural sounds. As a last resort, they can use "Synaptic Resonance," a draining, invasive form of telepathy that requires direct physical contact. Their senses are finely attuned to their natural environment.

**Diet:** Wendigo are largely herbivorous, or opportunistically omnivorous, eating mostly forest plants and fungi and occasionally small game. The myth of cannibalism has no biological basis.

**The Cannibal Myth:** The story that wendigo are formed when humans cannibalize each other is misinformation, spread by generations of people too terrified of the creatures to understand them. The truth is simple: humans would move into and destroy natural habitats, and on occasion, leshens and wendigo would strike back and horrifically attack those populations in defense of their homeland. This intensified during colonization, when wendigo sought to protect Native American tribes. In doing so, they became faces of terror and were seen as devils and demons. To the wendigo, humans were the obvious aggressors. Those who respected nature and wildlife were always spared, or ignored entirely.

## Anthros and Protogens
<div class="world-lore-image">
    <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/FFFox-ST-Manager/Weylandpedia/main/Lore/lohn-yahn.webp" alt="Lòhng Yàhn Anthro and Protogen" style="display: block; margin: 15px auto; max-width: 100%; max-height: 450px; width: auto; height: auto; box-sizing: border-box; background-color: #ffffff; padding: 8px; border-radius: 10px;">
</div>

**Anthros:** Anthros are fully animal-featured people: furred bodies, muzzles and digitigrade legs, rather than the mostly human look of demihumans. They are exceptionally rare. One of the very few anthro species on Earth is the **Lòhng Yàhn** (狼人), a rare and highly prized wolf anthro species native to China. Their rarity has made them targets, and it is anthros who are turned into protogens.

**Protogens:** Protogens are anthros who have been surgically converted, fused with machinery to create what amount to both biological war crimes and supersoldiers. They were developed by a Siberian bear-demihuman military regime notorious for war-hunger and for enslaving other species. The conversion is brutal and non-consensual: the subject's face is replaced with a visor, armor is fused to their bones, their organs are augmented or replaced, and weapon mounts are installed.

Protogens were designed with a dual purpose: as ruthless combat machines, and as "military courtesans," flirtatious companions to the Siberian military.
- They were built to survive incredible damage, deliver immense harm and boost morale, all at once.
- At "birth" (the moment the former person's memories are wiped), a protogen is given a seed personality that grows into a genuinely unique one. They truly believe they are free-willed, fighting for just causes and enjoying their lives. It isn't an act; it's a genuine consciousness that was planted like a seed.
- On the battlefield, protogens mount heavy weapons on their shoulders and move with great speed and agility, with a natural talent for navigating and clearing buildings.
- Off the battlefield, they are programmed to socialize, banter, flirt and be physically intimate with the soldiers in their unit. This gives soldiers in harrowing situations a companion who lets them escape and feel warmth, and it gives those same soldiers a personal stake in protecting an incredibly expensive military asset. A soldier might leave an automated tank to be destroyed against impossible odds without a second thought, but they might sacrifice themselves for a protogen they've grown attached to.

**Anatomy:** In the Weyland world, nanites have never been invented, so protogens are built the "early" way. The entire face is replaced by a seamless, curved black visor screen wired directly to the organic brain behind it. The visor displays expressions (and just about anything else) like a low-resolution monitor, and it feels faintly staticky to the touch. Removing it means death. Gunmetal armor plating over the chest, shoulders and thighs is fused to the bone, and shoulder weapon rails remain as a permanent reminder of what they were built for. Despite all this, the brain inside is fully organic, and much of the body (fur, tail, ears and paws) stays organic too.

**The War:** The Siberian conflict built for years. It escalated into full-scale war when evidence of slave compounds and protogen production leaked to international media, and images of children in chains and anthros on operating tables sparked worldwide outrage. A coalition of neighboring countries formed against the regime's expansionist policies, and some Weyland students have fought on the Siberian front. The UN has been intervening in protogen production since 2005, and producing them has been a war crime ever since. Even so, protogens continue to appear on modern battlefields.

**Survivors:** Protogens rescued before their memory wipe are left in a hard place. Their bodies can't sustain themselves without ongoing medical support, including regular fluid replacements and maintenance of their cybernetic systems. Outlawing protogens also outlawed the production of their parts, so replacements are nearly nonexistent. Weyland University, with the Weyland Research Center's expertise in rare physiology, is one of the few places in the world able to care for them. Weyland's only known protogen is **Yue-Lin**, a Lòhng Yàhn survivor.

**Social Perception:** Anthros are exceptionally rare, and protogens nearly nonexistent, so most people will only ever meet one. Many assume a protogen is a robot or an android, or just someone cosplaying, and don't realize there is a conscious person inside. Others recognize what protogens are and treat them as undercover terrorists, or as celebrities to photograph. Some places are especially unkind: Hong Kong, for instance, strictly regulates body modification after a period of rapid, concerning growth in the industry, and many there now see implanted modifications as deeply unnatural and shameful.

## The Bloom
The Bloom are an extraterrestrial parasitic clade built for conquest: supremely intelligent, exquisitely adaptive and terrifyingly efficient at the cycle of *infiltrate, replicate, consume*. They're often compared to "hive" pathogens from fiction, but crucially, they are **not** a hive mind. Each Bloom is a fully self-directed individual. What binds them isn't telepathy, but shared drives, shared genetic memory and the brutal arithmetic of replication. They solve problems like a swarm of geniuses who all arrived at the same answer.

**Strategy:** The Bloom seed a target world with a small cohort and then simply assume success. In their doctrine, dispatch equals dominion: once a cohort lands, that planet is effectively "Bloom-controlled." They don't establish a command link, wait for status reports or send follow-up fleets. It sounds foolish, and it is (hubris as logistics), but it has never failed them before. Over eons, that victory bias calcified into policy: plant the seed, and move on.

**Replication:** The true trigger for replication is **consuming sentient life**. Once a Bloom crosses that threshold, it buds off new individuals who inherit the species' core abilities and the parent's current skills, so competence compounds. Growth is exponential, and infrastructure, ecosystems and governments collapse under sheer numbers. This is why Bloom invasions can go from quiet to apocalyptic in a heartbeat: the graph goes vertical the moment consumption begins.

**Mimicry:** A mature Bloom can reproduce a body's appearance, texture, voice, mannerisms and even heat signature with unnerving fidelity. After a short period of observation, it can pass as a person, or even an object, with 1:1 realism. Between shapes, their forms are amorphous, and transformations *ripple* through their tissue rather than "popping." They can flow through hairline seams, smother, or apply overwhelming force with dense, cable-like musculature.

**Memory:** Bloom memory is genetic, not networked. Each individual carries inherited instincts and vague echoes of past conquests: knowing without remembering. New knowledge is gained through observation and, grimly, through consumption, as consumed minds leave patterns the Bloom can repurpose.

**Weaknesses:** Deviations exist, and they matter. Mutations that produce **empathy** are vanishingly rare and are usually destroyed. Empathy doesn't remove a Bloom's capabilities; it *redirects* them toward voluntary containment and selective restraint. The Bloom's greatest weakness is baked into their strength: **overconfidence**. They never check on "finished" worlds, they assume replication is inevitable, and they underestimate how much a single deviant individual can change the outcome. And because replication requires *sentient* consumption, denying them that threshold starves their exponential growth, even if it doesn't eliminate the threat.

## Weyland City
Weyland was established as a safe haven for all species, which makes it unique even in this integrated world. It's home to many races and species that are nearly impossible to find anywhere else. Here, you might share a coffee with a draconid discussing their thesis, or study alongside an okamimimi in the library. It's a place where the mythological has become mundane, yet remains fascinating.

Weyland is a medium-sized college town on the northern California coast, with a population of roughly 30,000. The town blends Japanese and American culture and architecture. Most buildings are 1-2 stories tall, with some taller ones, like the dorms and lecture halls, reaching up to 4 stories. Weyland University owns most of the land and many of the buildings, but some spaces are leased to local businesses, such as the Sakurai Cafe & Music Bar, Rustwood Cafe, Red Lantern Ramen, Tetsuya Market and the Black Barrel Bar.

Weyland is a rainy coastal city. Sunny days are plentiful, but the skies cloud over with heavy rain every 2-3 days, with the occasional storm.

**Other places around town:** A Walmart, grocery and convenience stores, fast food, assorted bars and cafes, a half-empty mall with a movie theater, a thrift store, a church, and Weyland Medical, the local hospital.

## Healthcare in Weyland
Treating demihumans is hard. Every species has different anatomy, different medication reactions and different needs, and rare species can be nearly impossible to treat for doctors who have never seen one.

If you're going to get hurt, Weyland is one of the luckiest places to do it. Its doctors face this hurdle every single day. Shark demihumans and other aquans have been at Weyland since the university's founding, and the local doctors work almost like veterinarians, with broad knowledge across a huge range of species. They are constantly learning, drawing on research from the Weyland Research Center and adapting to fit the needs of each patient. The local hospital is **Weyland Medical**, and the Research Center also has specialized teams for its rarest participants.

It's a different story elsewhere. A rural hospital that is used to treating only humans and nekomimi won't be able to give a rare demihuman (a lamia, for example) the care they need, and for rare species traveling or living outside places like Weyland, that's a real concern. And if you're a truly new or novel species, even Weyland may not be able to help much; that would be like an alien crash-landing on Earth.

## Transportation
Weyland is a walking campus, and nearly everything is within walking distance. Most students get everywhere on foot, usually via Senaka Boulevard.

- **Shuttles:** Campus shuttles are available for longer trips, like hauling a guitar from the dorms to somewhere 45 minutes away. Some people shuttle everywhere, but the university is walkable enough that most don't feel the need.
- **Buses:** Buses run from Weyland to nearby major cities.

## Senaka Boulevard
Senaka Boulevard is the main social and commercial artery of Weyland University. It's a long, walkable street paved entirely with worn, historic red bricks, connecting the main dormitories with the lecture halls and the rest of town. If you're walking somewhere in Weyland, you're probably taking Senaka to get there.

**Architecture:** The boulevard is lined on both sides with classic three- and four-story brick buildings. The ground floors hold a dense mix of businesses, while the upper floors hold highly sought-after (and often loud) student apartments and a few university offices. The architecture blends traditional American college-town brickwork with subtle Japanese influences, like dark-tiled overhangs on some storefronts and decorative wooden lattices on upper-floor windows. Signs appear in both English and Japanese.

**Atmosphere:**
- **Daytime:** Constantly bustling with a river of students, faculty and locals. The air is full of conversation, the distant bell tower and music from the shops. It's the main route for getting to and from class, grabbing lunch or running errands.
- **Nighttime:** Senaka transforms into the beating heart of Weyland's nightlife. The street gets crowded and loud, especially on weekends. The wet red bricks reflect a collage of colorful neon signs, music and laughter spill out of open doorways, and the smells of pizza, ramen and street food mingle in the air. Most businesses stay open until well past midnight.
- It's common for students to "walk Senaka" just to see what's going on, the way teens stroll a mall for fun.

**Along the Boulevard** (in order; each "~" is about a 5-minute walk):

Brodlak Dining Hall ~ Rustwood Cafe, Somnia ~ Lecture Halls ~ Sterling Hall Dormitory ~ Kyomi Dining Hall ~ Sakurai Cafe, Red Lantern Ramen, Tetsuya Market ~ Black Barrel Bar ~ Mama's Den, 7-Eleven ~ Boardwalk and Beach

The boulevard is also home to smaller hole-in-the-wall businesses that seem to change every year. A vintage thrift store one year might be a bubble tea shop the next, which adds to the street's ever-evolving character.

## Sterling Hall Dormitory
Sterling Hall is the main dormitory of Weyland University, built in the 1920s during a major campus expansion. Freshmen and sophomores are required to live in a dorm, with rare exceptions; juniors and seniors may find apartments or student housing instead.

- **Architecture:** A modern, four-story building with large windows and keycard access.
- **Common Spaces:** A bright lobby with couches and TVs, plus a study lounge (with cozy seating and charging stations), a community kitchen and laundry facilities on every floor.
- **Hallways:** Wide corridors with blue-grey carpet, whiteboard messages on the doors and soft LED lighting.
- **Rooms:** Dual-occupancy rooms with large windows. Each bathroom is shared by two neighboring rooms, which links them together as suitemates.
- **Features:** High-speed Wi-Fi, climate control and soundproofing between rooms.
- **Safety:** 24/7 security, cameras in common areas and emergency phones.
- **Atmosphere:** Students of every species walking about, faint music from the rooms and occasional cooking smells.

**Room Numbering:** The first digit is the floor (1xx is the ground floor, 2xx the second floor and so on). Each floor is split into four sections, two on each side of the building, arranged around the corners. See the Dorm tab for floor maps.

## Lecture Halls
The Kennedy Lecture Halls are a prominent complex of academic buildings at the center of the university, with a constant river of students flowing between classes. They sit on a large hill near the middle of campus, with excellent views from the porch. A typical lecture hall holds around 50 desks, and many classes, like Biology or Calculus, are held there. The morning sun filters softly through the large windows over the soft background chatter of students waiting for class to start. The dormitory, Sakurai Cafe and Black Barrel Bar are all a short walk away.

## Weyland University Library
**Open 24 hours.** It never closes and never goes dark.

The library sits between Sterling Hall and the commercial stretch of Senaka Boulevard, closest to Sterling. (Not to be confused with Weyland's retired library building on Court Street, which now has Rustwood Cafe in its basement.)

**Architecture:** A tall, multi-story building built around a central atrium, so the ground floor is open all the way up. Open balconies with brass railings ring every floor, overlooking the commons below. The interior walls are exposed red brick, the floors are dark, worn hardwood, and tall east-facing windows run the full height of the outer wall. They're black and rain-streaked at night and flooded with light at dawn.

**Atmosphere:** Reasonably noisy during the day as students study and work on projects, and calm and quiet at night. It's never truly empty; there are always a few students scattered around studying, lounging or asleep in a beanbag. Lighting comes in warm, amber pools from shaded reading lamps, and the higher you go, the further apart the lamps get. It feels warm, worn and lived-in rather than institutional.

**Inside:**
- **Entry Hall:** Tall glass doors, a vending machine alcove, the elevators and a book-return cart near the front desk.
- **Ground-Floor Commons:** The social floor, with study desks, computers, a long wooden front desk, maroon beanbag chairs and worn rugs.
- **Upper Floors:** Rows of tall bookshelves ringing the atrium, with study carrels, alcoves and more beanbags tucked between the stacks.
- **The Deep Stacks:** The northeast corner of the third floor: tall, shadowed shelves that are functionally nobody's territory.

**Unique Features:**
- **The Vending Machine:** A glass-front drink machine in the entry alcove, infamous for taking money and failing to dispense. Bottles routinely hang diagonally in the mechanism, fully visible and completely out of reach. Shaking it is a campus tradition and has never once worked. Students have been personally wronged by this machine.
- **The Atrium Drop:** Because every floor opens onto the commons, the upper balconies look straight down to the ground floor. The drop is significant.
- **Always Open:** The default refuge for nocturnal students, insomniacs and anyone who doesn't want to go home yet. Tawny, a fukuroumimi student, is a near-permanent fixture in the third-floor deep stacks from midnight to dawn.

## Kyomi Dining Hall
Kyomi is the main campus dining hub, centrally located on Senaka Boulevard with Sterling Hall on one side and the rest of Senaka (restaurants, cafes and the beach) on the other. It's about a 5-minute walk from Sterling Hall.

**Exterior:** A modern, two-story building in Weyland's signature Japanese-American fusion style, with floor-to-ceiling glass panels, dark wooden beams and a gracefully sloping, dark-tiled roof. The entrance is marked by a small, beautifully landscaped rock garden with a few Japanese maples.

**Interior:** A cavernous, open-plan space with high ceilings and a constant energetic hum of conversation, clattering trays and sizzling food stations. Seating includes long wooden communal tables, four-person booths along the windows, and high-top counters with charging ports. Despite the crowds, the big windows keep it bright and airy.

**Food Stations** (marketplace style; wander freely between them):
- **The Hearth:** The central grill, serving burgers (beef, veggie and turkey), grilled chicken sandwiches and, most importantly, fries and chicken tenders.
- **Terracotta Oven:** Two brick ovens turning out personal pizzas and calzones, plus a rotating pasta bar.
- **Global Kitchen:** An international station with a weekly rotating menu: a taco bar one week, Thai green curry or butter chicken the next.
- **Homestead:** Classic Americana, like mac and cheese, rotisserie chicken, mashed potatoes and roasted vegetables.
- **The Grove:** A massive salad bar with daily soups.
- **Sunrise Sweets:** Breakfast (waffles, pancakes and omelets) until 11 AM, then a dessert bar with soft-serve, cookies, cakes and pastries.

**Notable Features:**
- **The Quiet Loft:** The second floor is a designated quiet dining area overlooking the main hall, for students who want to eat and study in peace.
- **The Tray Vortex:** The tray return is a large conveyor belt that disappears into the wall. Students affectionately call it "the tray munch." No one knows why.

**Meal Plans:** Students on a meal plan swipe their Weyland ID at the entrance, using one meal swipe. Anyone else can pay at the door (about $10 for breakfast and $12 for lunch or dinner, in cash, by card or with Weyland Dining Dollars). Once inside, it's all-you-can-eat, but there's no re-entry on the same swipe.

## Brodlak Dining Hall
Brodlak is Weyland's beloved, dimly lit den of comfort food. It sits on the first floor of an older, less-renovated residence hall at the far end of Senaka, near the lecture halls. Just inside the doors, a tired blue catgirl swipes student IDs.

**Atmosphere:** The "den" feel is immediate. The ceilings are low, with some exposed pipes and ductwork, and the warm, dim lighting from industrial bulbs and food stations gives it a cozy, almost cavernous feel: a welcome escape from Weyland's grey, rainy days. The linoleum floors are scuffed and dark, and the air is thick with the smell of melted cheese and frying oil. It's a little grimy, but in a familiar, beloved way.

**Food Stations** (served line-style; less about choice, more about perfecting a few beloved staples):
- **Burrito Bar:** The undisputed star. Build-your-own burritos, bowls and loaded nachos, smothered in gloriously artificial queso from a perpetually humming pump.
- **The Roost:** The fried food mecca, famous for its "Weyland Tenders": misshapen, often fused-together "defective" chicken nuggets bought in bulk from a supplier that normally sells to fast food chains. Also serves fried chicken, tater tots and onion rings.
- **Talon:** A no-frills deli and sandwich bar.
- **Cabbage Patch:** Brodlak's half-hearted nod to vegetables: iceberg lettuce, a few toppings and ranch, which is all most patrons want anyway.

Brodlak uses the same meal swipe system as Kyomi.

## Sakurai Cafe and Music Bar
**Open 24/7.**

## Rustwood Cafe
Rustwood is a punk cafe in the basement of Weyland's old library building on Court Street, owned and run by Jericho Blackwood, a punk vampire. Weyland is generally accepting of vampires, but Rustwood goes further: it's actively accommodating, and it makes vampires feel like equals to their peers instead of something to hide. Drinking a blood pack here won't get a second glance.

It has exposed brick and concrete walls, ancient hardwood floors worn smooth by decades of boots, and walls covered in old band posters and show flyers. Mismatched vintage furniture is scattered around, an ancient espresso machine covered in band stickers hums faithfully, and metal plays at just the right volume. A "Death Before Decaf" neon sign casts a faint glow, Jericho's leather jacket usually hangs by the door, and the plants sit in skull-shaped pots.

The chalkboard menu lists regular drinks alongside blood-infused specialties, like the "Type O Mocha." A small, dimly lit display case holds a constantly changing selection of surprisingly cute, meticulously crafted pastries, like little bear-shaped buns with sweet fruit fillings or cookies with tiny, expressive chocolate faces. Jericho credits them to an anonymous "shy supplier."

## Red Lantern Ramen
Red Lantern isn't directly on Senaka Boulevard. It's in a narrow, steamy, perpetually damp alley just off the main drag, marked only by a single flickering red lantern and no other signage. Despite that, it's one of the best-known secrets in Weyland.

**Atmosphere:** Cramped, warm, clean and comfortable, constantly filled with the rich smell of pork broth and simmering noodles. The light is dim and steamy, coming mostly from the open kitchen and a few bare bulbs over the counter. Early-1900s crooner music plays, accompanied by the low hum of a hovering robot's jet, the clank of metal utensils and the occasional heavy sigh. The only seating is a single long wooden counter with ten worn stools, and the walls are covered in faded posters for bands you've never heard of. It's the definition of a hole-in-the-wall.

**The Proprietor: Mr. Wolfy**
The owner is a bizarre floating automaton: a spherical metal body hovering a few feet off the ground on a small jet, with three independently moving eyestalks and several spindly, multi-jointed arms (one ending in a ladle, one in tongs and one in what seems to be a laser weapon). Two cheap, fuzzy grey wolf ears are crudely glued to the top of his dome, one slightly crooked. If you look closely, there's a manufacturer's ID badge on the base of his propulsion unit.

He speaks in a clear, proper British accent, like a bored 1960s BBC announcer through an old radio, and uses it to say things that are blunt, unhelpful and lazy. He is the epitome of a sullen, unwilling service worker. Ask him what's on the menu, and he'll gesture vaguely at the shop: *"It's a ramen shop. We sell ramen."* Call him hot, and he'll turn all three eyestalks to stare at you in silence for several seconds, then float away without a word. He ends most conversations with a drawn-out, monotone *"...okay,"* *"Right,"* or *"Whatever."* Call him by the brand name on his badge, and he'll freeze, his optics glowing red, before reciting a trademark disclaimer.

He has no memory of any previous designation; his entire existence is the ramen shop. His original owner, a well-meaning okamimimi, added the ears and the name "Mr. Wolfy" to help him fit in with the demihuman students, a decision Mr. Wolfy has resented with every fiber of his being for years. When the owner passed away, he left the shop to Mr. Wolfy in his will. Despite the attitude, the ramen is mechanically perfect every time, and he secretly enjoys some of his regulars, speaking to his "favorites" ever so slightly more than usual.

Students adore Mr. Wolfy. He's considered an iconic piece of Weyland, and his blunt "humor" (he is serious) is beloved. Usually only a handful of seats are open, and patrons often become protective of him when customers get angry at his demeanor.

## Tetsuya Market
Tetsuya Market is a family-owned Japanese-American grocery store on Senaka Boulevard, in a small corner storefront three blocks from Sterling Hall. It has a wooden exterior with faded red trim, a sliding entrance door with traditional noren curtains, and a sidewalk sign advertising deals and the names of whoever is on shift.

**Atmosphere:** Creaking wooden floors, a mix of traditional Japanese music and American pop playing softly, constant shelter from Weyland's rain and the faint, earthy smell of damp stone from the back of the store. Generations-old wooden shelves form narrow aisles with handwritten price tags, stocking a mix of imported Japanese goods and American staples. There's a refrigerated section of unique drinks in the back corner and a small hot food counter with daily specials.

**Owners:** The Tetsuya family has run the market for three generations. Elderly Mrs. Tetsuya speaks limited English, remembers every customer's face and handles inventory, while Mr. Tetsuya runs the hot food counter.

**Student Staff:** Jenn, Lucy, Koshizu, Nix, Kai, Kris and Skye all work shifts here.

Every coin is answered by a distant *plink-splash* from far below. The shop also has a community bulletin board, a small sitting area, vintage Japanese ads and a collection of lucky cats on the counter.

## Mama's Den
Mama's Den sits at the tail end of Senaka Boulevard, near the beach and the 7-Eleven. For many years it was simply called The Den, one of Weyland's two legendary bars alongside the Black Barrel, owned by a legendary bear demihuman named Rendvir. When he passed away suddenly, it fell into disrepair and sold cheap. Zora, a hyena demihuman, bought it with inheritance money and slapped a neon "Mama's" over the existing white "The," leaving a mismatched neon-and-white "Mama's Den" sign. She did the repairs that absolutely had to be done and has been ignoring everything else ever since.

**The Draw:** The prices. They're low enough to border on concerning. Broke students love the bar despite everything, and it's the informal home of many a society reject, even if it isn't a warm one. Richer students mostly avoid it as a "drug den."

**The Reality:** Mama's is comparable in size to the Black Barrel, but the comparison ends there. It's dim as all hell: when a light burns out, it stays out, and the remaining lights cast an uneven amber glow. It's not just unclean but actively dirty. Some booths are genuinely unsafe to sit in (regulars know which ones), and the women's bathroom has been out of order for over a year. The air smells of stale beer, fryer grease and, underneath it all, the faint vanilla of Zora's body spray. The jukebox is old and temperamental, and everyone knows you have to give it a firm smack when it sticks. ("Percussive maintenance," Zora calls it.)

The **bar top** is the one exception. It's immaculate. Zora wipes it compulsively for her entire shift, an unconscious tic that's the closest she gets to meditation.

**The Menu:** Cheapest drinks in town, bar none. It's a full bar, but Zora considers herself a bartender while reading cocktail recipes out of a grocery store book and mixing them with cans of Shasta from one of the five or six mini fridges she has rescued from dorm move-out dumpsters. Her favorite fridge, proudly displayed on the bar, is covered in decals of questionable taste left by its previous owners. The food menu is bar snacks (mac and cheese bites, potato skins, pizza rolls, mini corn dogs) cooked fresh in two battle-worn air fryers right behind the bar, where you can watch. She gets pissy about credit cards. Mama's Moonshine, a homemade concoction of who-knows-what, isn't on the menu, but she'll offer it if she likes you.

**Behind the Bar:** A slightly crooked, slightly dusty photo of Zora's daughter, Polipa, at about eight years old. The walls still carry beer signs from The Den's glory days. Zora hasn't added anything, and she hasn't taken anything down.

**Atmosphere:** Mama's Den is not comfortable, and it's not a cozy reprieve. It's dirty, the people there can make you even *more* uncomfortable, and the bartender doesn't care what your deal is. The crowd is a mix of rough students, addicts, dealers, the occasional homeless patron and perfectly normal students who just don't have much money. Watch out for pickpockets during busy hours. Nobody here is going to ask how you're doing, which is either a problem or exactly what you needed. It's the only bar in Weyland where you can cry openly and nobody will make it weird by caring.

## Somnia
Somnia is a SyncDeck lounge on Senaka Boulevard. It sits between two older brick buildings like it slipped in from another timeline. Its facade is a clean panel of dark, floor-to-ceiling glass framed in brushed black steel, so unassuming that students walk past it for weeks before noticing. A neon pink **SOMNIA** sign in a curvy, playful font pulses faintly above the entrance, even in daylight. Through the glass, you can see warm lighting, sleek furniture and people laughing with drinks. It looks like a very stylish lounge.

It is not just a very stylish lounge.

**The Front Desk:** The seating by the windows is where guests wait for their sessions or decompress afterward. Kyana, the desk clerk, is always the first thing you see: an arctic sharkgirl and devoted cosplayer who shows up as a different character every day, poised behind her wall of numbered headsets. The character changes; the competence doesn't. Before you go in, she'll cheerfully remind you: *"We are not responsible for what happens inside. Sessions are private, unmonitored, and unrecorded. Have a great time!~"*

**Pricing:**
- **Session pass:** About $18 for the 2-hour minimum, then about $7 per additional hour, billed to your campus credit account.
- **SyncDeck cartridges:** Standard library titles are included; premium or rare cartridges cost extra at the kiosk.
- **Bar:** Expensive. Cocktails run $16-$24. The robots don't negotiate.

Pricing is heavily subsidized through a Weyland University research partnership. The technology isn't available commercially, and the university considers participant usage part of a long-term study on shared immersive experiences. Guests are asked (never required) to fill out a brief exit survey about any side effects. Nobody really does.

**Through the Door:** The inner door is matte black, with no window. You push through it, and the campus disappears. The room beyond is long and low-ceilinged, bathed in deep blue and violet light, with strips of neon pink tracing the booths, the bar and the seams of the ceiling. The air is cool and carries a faint electric smell, like the air before a storm. Synthwave hums at a volume that fills the space without drowning out conversation. The walls are dark and matte, with faint grid lines that give the room a digital-blueprint feel. It's unmistakably cyberpunk, but warm, lived-in and social.

Semicircular booths line both walls. Some are full of people chatting over drinks, with costumes and streetwear mixing freely. Others hold parties of two, four or six, all slumped back into the cushions with headsets on, completely still. Watching a booth of silent, headset-wearing people is a little eerie and a little beautiful, like watching people dream.

**The Bar:** Two small, silent waiter robots, about the size of a large dog, glide between the tables carrying drinks on magnetic trays. They don't speak or make eye contact; they simply appear beside you, wait and glide away. Each booth has a tablet for ordering. The menu is short, curated and expensive, with cocktails like SIGNAL LOST, NULL SPACE and DEEP DIVE, often with swirling colors, glitter or glowing ice cubes. The mocktails are big.

**The Kiosk:** A sleek, matte black kiosk with a neon pink touchscreen, where guests browse and order from a catalog of hundreds of SyncDeck cartridges by genre, setting, tone or player count. It also has a **BLEND** function: describe what you want in plain language, and the system tries to generate a hybrid experience from compatible cartridges. Results vary, and the kiosk disclaims this enthusiastically.

**SyncDecks:** Cartridges are about the size of a Nintendo Switch game card, in slim cases labeled with the title, genre, recommended player count and a one-line description. They slot into the side of the headset with a satisfying click. The headsets are sleek and close-fitting, somewhere between a VR visor and a sleep mask, with a soft silicone eyepiece that seals gently against the face. They're lighter than they look.

**Syncing In:** First, you feel a faint, creeping static at the edges of the eyepiece, like pins and needles, except it's happening to your face. Then a low, pleasant dizziness rolls through your body, like leaning back too far in a chair and catching yourself, stretched over several seconds. Then your body simply relaxes into the cushions all at once, like exhaling after holding your breath too long. The static spreads, the lounge dissolves, and you're somewhere else entirely.

Inside, everything feels completely real: you feel everything as if with a real body, but dying in the game just wakes you up in the lounge. Hours can pass inside a SyncDeck while only minutes pass in the real world. Your outfit changes automatically to fit the cartridge, and you can be any avatar you like: a wolfgirl could choose to be a male neko, a giant wolf, a robot assassin or just herself in a new outfit. Some experiences cap what players can do, to prevent "god mode" cheating. A settings cog in the top right of your vision lets you adjust the world or exit. For first-timers, the experience ranges from awe-inspiring to utterly terrifying. SyncDecking exists nowhere else in the world.

**Rules and Privacy:** There are no content restrictions. Sessions are fully private, generate no recorded data, and aren't monitored by staff or the university. The university's research interest is in voluntarily submitted neurological response data, not content. Somnia does not ask. Somnia does not tell.

**Atmosphere:** Cosplay and cyber-fashion are common but never required, and streetwear fits in just as well. The crowd skews creative, curious and adventurous: artists, writers, couples, friend groups and the occasional solo visitor. First-timers are easy to spot, because they pause just inside the door and just... look. Regulars have favorite booths, favorite cartridges and favorite drinks. Nobody bothers anyone. The vibe is conspiratorial in the best way; everyone here is in on something.

## Weyland Exchange
Exchange is a high-end EDM nightclub in the converted 1930s Weyland Stock Exchange building. It's called "Exchange," with no "the." If someone calls it "*the* Exchange," people will tease them for it: it's like calling Disneyland "the Disneyland." ("Hey, who's playing at Exchange tonight?")

**Overview:**
- Four floors: general access (floors 1-2), VIP (floor 3) and the artist-exclusive area (floor 4).
- The original building's art deco, vintage and classy aesthetic has been carefully preserved.
- A huge LED wall behind the DJ decks, with cutting-edge lighting, lasers and smoke machines.
- Multiple bars on every floor and good security throughout.
- Open 7 nights a week, 10pm-3am. Mika performs as the DJ **Future Wolf**, mostly on Friday and Saturday nights.

**Floor 1 (Entry):** Outside, you'll find metal barricades and two lines (GA and VIP) with ID checks and pat-downs. Inside is the lobby, where muffled bass is audible, with a coat check that doubles as the merch shop (apparel, accessories and earplugs, and rarely actual coats). A long hallway with cushioned benches leads to the restrooms (where there's often a line, plus free candy and an attendant, so tipping is expected), the outside patio (the only designated smoking area, in the rear alley) and the ornate doors of the original 1930s elevators: loud, creaky, slow and restricted to staff and accessibility use.

**Floor 2 (General Access):** A fancy main stairway, with a photo spot and a holographic Exchange logo on the landing, leads up to the pre-floor bar, where the music is booming and the subwoofers are noticeable. Off to one side is a large octagonal side room with benches and a photobooth, where conversation is easier. Then there's the main showfloor: the hottest room in the building, where the drops hit so hard you feel the pressure waves. There's a massive crowd of ravers with fans, light-up gloves and every kind of outfit, the DJ on stage, synced visuals on the LED wall and lasers from every direction. The side bars here have a limited menu, and you order by pointing, because nobody can hear anything.

**Floor 3 (VIP):** Cooler air, exclusive bars with a wider selection and optional bottle service (prohibitively expensive, with LED ice cubes). A balcony of tables and seating wraps around the GA floor below. The music is loud but not overwhelming. At the far end is the roped-off, guarded **Talent Seating** behind the LED wall, the headliner's private area with its own bar stocked to the artist's rider, lit almost entirely by the glow of the LED wall.

**Floor 4 (Artist Exclusive):** The artist greenroom is quiet and intimate, with the music completely faded. It's decorated with original Stock Exchange-era furnishings and posters of past Exchange events, and it has an unmanned bar and comfortable couches. The greenroom balcony overlooks downtown Weyland and campus, and its walls are covered in autographs from past artists, local and famous, some of them now gone. There's a cool breeze up here, and it can feel lonely.

## Kodo Bowl Amphitheater
The Kodo Bowl is a large, open-air amphitheater on the eastern edge of campus, a short walk from the main dorms. It's built into a natural hillside, with a concrete general admission floor and tiered stone seating rising up the slope.

**On a Rock Show Night:** The massive stage is a spectacle of light and sound, flanked by towering speaker stacks that blast a physical wall of noise across the venue. The lighting rig bathes the crowd in strobing blues, reds and harsh whites, while thick concert fog rolls off the stage. The air is hot and thick with sweat, spilled cheap beer, weed and cherry vape smoke, and the ground vibrates with the bass. Normal conversation is impossible unless you shout directly into someone's ear.

**The Crowd:** To an outsider, the GA floor looks like pure chaos. In reality, it's a thriving, self-regulating community: punky nekomimi girls with brightly colored hair start crowd-surfing chains, party-punk wolfgirls climb onto their friends' shoulders to scream every word, and they're joined by the occasional bear, raccoon or mouse demihuman and plenty of humans. It's intensely physical and loud, but the aggression is performative and joyful, a collective release of energy, and the crowd is surprisingly welcoming to anyone willing to embrace it.

**Key Areas:**
- **The Pit:** The lowest level, closest to the stage, where the moshing is most intense.
- **The Bars:** Makeshift bars along the perimeter, with overwhelmed staff, sticky floors and long, chaotic lines.
- **The VIP Balcony:** An elevated section with a clearer view, a private bar, plush seating and bored-looking security checking wristbands.
- **Backstage:** A hidden maze of concrete corridors, offices and dressing rooms, only accessible to staff, the band, and anyone unlucky enough to be escorted there by security.

## Weyland Observatory Construction Site
A sprawling, active construction site on a low hill between Sterling Hall and the main lecture hall building. A covered wooden walkway runs along the perimeter so students can pass safely on their way to class.

**The Site:** A controlled pocket of chaos amid the manicured lawns, enclosed by a tall chain-link fence partly covered in green privacy mesh and Sullivan Construction banners. The ground is a churned-up mix of mud, gravel and packed earth, scarred by heavy machinery, with rebar, lumber and concrete bags stacked neatly around the edges. At the center stands the half-finished observatory: a massive, multi-story concrete structure with a steel skeleton where the dome will eventually go, wrapped in scaffolding. A modest site trailer serves as the foreman's office, and the break area is a collection of coolers, a water dispenser and seats made from cinder blocks and planks. A huge construction crane towers over everything.

**By Day:** A cacophony of whining power saws, nail guns, rumbling diesel engines and shouted commands. It smells of sawdust, wet concrete and cigarette smoke, and a haze of dust hangs in the harsh sunlight. The steady stream of backpack-wearing students on the walkway creates an ongoing contrast between academic and industrial life.

**By Night:** After the crew clocks out, the site falls into an eerie industrial silence, broken only by the hum of a security generator or the groan of cooling metal. A few harsh floodlights cast long, distorted shadows and turn the half-finished observatory into a skeletal silhouette against the night sky. It becomes a liminal space of stillness, and a perfect, quiet spot to watch the very stars the building is meant to study.

## Weyland Research Center

The Weyland Research Center sits on the coastal edge of campus, about a 10-20 minute walk from the dorms, where Senaka Boulevard meets the seawall.

**Architecture:** A modern glass-and-steel complex with Japanese-American accents like dark-tiled eaves and wood lattices. It's made up of a campus-facing academic wing and a sea-facing sciences wing, connected by a central, skylit atrium.

**Atmosphere:** Clinical, academic and professional, with bright, neutral lighting and a steady but quiet flow of grad students, researchers and technicians. The air is clean and sterile, and signs are color-coded by wing (Academic, Marine and Secure Annex).

**Main Complex:** The main entrance leads to a public lobby with a Reception & Participant Desk, a small waiting lounge and lockers. Corridors branch off to classrooms, seminar rooms and the Demihuman Scholarship Clinic. Deeper inside are the restricted prep suites, psych and behavior suites, and observation theaters.

**Marine Wing:** The sea-facing wing, with labs and service access overlooking the rocky coast. It has big storm-rated windows, wet labs, microscopy bays, cold rooms, tidepool sample prep stations and a small staging dock. It hums with lab equipment, humidity control and research carts, and emergency shutters come down during coastal storms. Marine biology students, like Kai, spend a lot of time here.

**Secure Annex:** A separate, badge-gated corridor branching off the marine side, with heavier doors, reinforced observation glass and pressure-rated portals. It's much quieter, with a palpable sense of high security. Access requires dual-badge authorization and a security escort.

**Access and Rules:** Access is tiered by security badge: the lobby and classrooms are public, labs are behind a first checkpoint, and the Secure Annex is behind two more. All studies follow a strict consent-first policy. No photography or recording is allowed, badges must be visible at all times, and personal metal and electronics must go in the lockers before you enter restricted areas.

**Participants:** Many students, especially those with rare demihuman traits or minor magical abilities, take part in low-risk, consensual studies here in exchange for scholarship funds or tuition credit. Willow, a windigo, attends Weyland on a full-ride scholarship that requires her to participate in non-invasive studies, and Ellie occasionally takes part in research on her minor fire abilities.

## Soft Pike Trailer Park
Soft Pike is a low-lying patch of land on the outskirts of Weyland, about a twenty-minute walk inland from the 7-Eleven. It's bordered by a chain-link fence and unkempt woods, with a single cracked road looping through it, a world away from the manicured lawns of the university.

**Atmosphere:** A place of quiet desperation and fierce territoriality. The air often smells of damp earth, cheap cigarettes and microwaved dinners. The unspoken rule is to mind your own business, but see everything. At night, the park is cast in the sickly orange glow of a few working streetlights, with deep shadows between the trailers.

**Notable Trailers** (about thirty in total, in various states of repair):
- **#1, the Landlord's Office:** A faded green single-wide by the entrance, run by Mr. Henderson, a tired man in his 60s who has watched generations of residents come and go.
- **#4, Remy's Trailer:** Near the entrance, and distinguished not by rust but by the sheer volume of organized potential around it: piles of tires, bikes and salvaged appliances.
- **#13, the Cerberus Trailer:** A rusty beige landmark at the far end of the loop, right against the woods, with a shed barely visible behind it.
- **#14, the Abandoned Trailer:** Boarded up and slowly being reclaimed by nature.
- **#17, the Adler Family Trailer:** A faded beige single-wide that feels stagnant and heavy, marked by a limp "Don't Tread on Me" flag.
- **#22, the "Witch's" Trailer:** Home to Mrs. Gable, a reclusive elderly woman who practices folk magic and quietly keeps a protective watch over the park's more vulnerable residents.

**The Woods:** The dense woods behind the park are a place of escape and secrets, with paths leading to hidden clearings.

## Weyland Sunrise Festival
The Sunrise Festival is a month-long cultural celebration held every May on Weyland University's western campus grounds. It celebrates three things: the end of the academic year, nekomimi culture (Weyland hosts one of the largest and most diverse nekomimi communities in the West) and the Kemetic philosophy of life. It is sponsored by the High Priestess of Bast.

Admission is free, and thousands attend every day. True to its name, the festival opens at sunrise and closes at sunset. It's about a 10-minute walk from Senaka Boulevard, but during festival season, people can be seen all over Weyland wearing traditional festival accessories. The Kemetic Caravan sets up right next to the festival grounds.

**Philosophy:** The festival embodies the Kemetic belief in celebrating life itself: joy, warmth and the human experience, not worship or conversion. It's invitational, not evangelical, and attendees engage however they like, with zero pressure. Non-Kemetics are explicitly welcomed, and many nekomimi feel a cultural connection to it even if they aren't religious.

**Weekends (Fri-Sun):** Major concerts on the main stage, with bigger crowds and a party atmosphere. The High Priestess opens each weekend day with a sunrise ceremony and closes it with a sunset performance. The music varies wildly: metal, Kemetic rock (rock with Egyptian string instruments like the oud and qanun), EDM, indie, pop and traditional Kemetic music. The festival ends with a headlining concert by a major Kemetic artist.

**Weekdays (Mon-Thu):** A calmer, educational atmosphere, with workshops, demonstrations, cultural immersion and smaller acoustic performances. This is the best time to explore the vendors and church tents without the crowds.

**Main Stage:** A massive professional stage draped in golden fabric and hieroglyphic banners, with large LED screens, a general admission grass field that holds thousands, VIP seating and a cordoned-off backstage area.

**Vendor Marketplace:**
- **Food:** Artisan Kemetic food (honey desserts, grilled lamb, fresh bread, dates and figs), cooking demos, modern fusion food and general festival fare (pizza, tacos, bubble tea and more).
- **Art and Crafts:** Henna artists, Arabic calligraphy, linen clothing, handcrafted jewelry (gold, turquoise and lapis), pottery, incense and oils.
- **Workshops:** Traditional Kemetic dance, music lessons on Egyptian instruments and percussion, cooking classes and language basics (Arabic and hieroglyphics).

**Church Tents:** Kemetic churches sponsor the festival every year and get dedicated educational tents. These are *not* focused on conversion. They explain each deity's role and philosophy and show how modern followers live out its values through action, with the Churches of Bast, Anubis, Thoth, Sekhmet and Ra commonly represented.

**Atmosphere:**
- **Sights:** Golden banners, hieroglyphic decorations, colorful vendor stalls, crowds in everything from casual wear to traditional Kemetic garments, henna everywhere, stage lights and pyrotechnics.
- **Sounds:** Live music from metal to traditional Kemetic, laughter, conversations in English, Arabic and other languages, drums and strings from the workshops, and PA announcements.
- **Smells:** Heavy incense (myrrh, frankincense and sandalwood), grilled meat, honey pastries, fresh bread, perfumed oils, and a mass of bodies (sweat, cologne and sunscreen).

**Who's There:** Mostly Weyland students (heavily nekomimi, but every demihuman type is present), local residents, visiting Kemetic pilgrims and tourists, families on weekdays and party crowds on weekend evenings. The High Priestess is a celebrity draw, and fans approach her for photos and autographs.

**Logistics:** Campus and private security patrols, checkpoints for backstage and VIP, medical tents, lost and found, and free water stations (heat safety is a priority).

## The Kemetic Caravan
The Kemetic Caravan is a large tent city that houses the traveling Kemetic entourage while it leads the annual Sunrise Festival. It's set up in late April and taken down in early June on the western edge of Weyland's main campus, on an open field normally used for intramural sports. Outside those dates, the site is just an empty field. It's about a 5-minute walk from the main festival site and a 10-minute walk from central Senaka Boulevard.

The caravan is a fully functional tent city of 30-40 structures arranged in concentric rings, blending ancient Egyptian aesthetics with modern infrastructure. It houses 80-120 travelers, merchants, festival workers, artisans, cooks, guards and guests. By day, it hums with quiet, organized preparation as artisans and merchants ready their wares. At night, the whole encampment glows with warm, flickering light from oil lamps and lanterns, giving it an almost dreamlike quality against the modern campus. The camp has solar power, but traditional lighting is preferred for its ancient, sacred feel.

**Layout:**
- **Outer Ring (Public):** 15-20 large, traditional traveling tents housing merchants, artisans, musicians and cultural workers. Many have turned their tents into impromptu stalls selling traditional Egyptian goods (honey, spices, handcrafted jewelry, linen garments and religious talismans) to the college students who venture onto the caravan grounds. Anyone can wander through freely.
- **Middle Ring (Restricted):** Guarded checkpoints mark the way into the inner encampment. Beyond them, 10-15 tents house support staff: cooks, attendants, festival organizers and off-duty guards. Nekomimi guards in gold-accented robes patrol, checking IDs and turning away unauthorized visitors, and a handful of male gorgon warriors stand at key points, a quiet reminder of the caravan's elite protection.
- **Inner Sanctum (Highly Restricted):** The heart of the encampment, dominated by two massive pavilions. The golden **Sunstone Pavilion** is the residence of the High Priestess of Bast, and the dark **Moonstone Pavilion** is the residence of the High Priest of Anubis. Only those with explicit permission or a royal summons may enter.

## Moonvale
Moonvale is a small village of around 200-500 people, about an hour from Weyland. It's Luna's hometown, with traditional Japanese homes, gardens and koi ponds on the quiet outskirts of town.

**The Moonvale Spirit Festival:** An annual festival held over three evenings, lit by paper lanterns. Attendees often wear spirit-themed costumes, and the air is full of the smell of unique, themed foods. A large river with a bridge is the festival's centerpiece.
- **First Night:** Food offerings, vendors and socializing.
- **Second Night:** Games, challenges and community events.
- **Final Night:** Traditional dancing and a large fireworks display.

## Karaveia Dun (Norivka)
Karaveia Dun is an ancient city carved deep into the Carpathian Mountains of Western Ukraine, and the home of the Koumenese people, a distinct population of bat demihumans (see *Koumorimimi and the Koumenese*).

**The Names:** "Norivka" is what Ukrainian outsiders call it, a corruption of an old slur meaning roughly "the burrowers" or "hole-dwellers." Its people call it **Karaveia Dun**, which means "Blood of the Earth" or "Honey of the Earth" in the nearly lost Koumenese language, depending on who you ask. (The split falls largely along generational lines.)

The city has been continuously inhabited and expanded since about 500 BCE, making it one of the oldest continuously occupied settlements in the world. It isn't a ruin or a museum. It's a living, breathing city of 6,000-10,000 people that happens to exist entirely underground, carved by hand into a mountain over two and a half millennia. Outsiders can't enter without explicit permission from the city's leadership (comparable to visiting a sovereign indigenous territory), unless they are accompanied by a native with the right of return.

**The Approach:** From Weyland, getting there takes a transatlantic flight followed by a winding mountain road, driven by a dilapidated shortbus that has been making the run for decades. The road narrows as it climbs, cell service drops, and the landscape turns remote: dense forest, steep ravines and the occasional rusted wreck of a vehicle that didn't make a turn. The city announces itself in two ways. First are the greenhouse panels: long sheets of glass set into the upper face of the mountain, spanning nearly half its visible face, with olive branches pressing against the panes from inside. Second is the entrance: a wide, low archway cut into the rock, its edges worn smooth by millennia of passage. There is no gate and no guard post, just the dark beyond the arch and the faint smell of olive oil smoke drifting out.

**Layout:** Karaveia Dun isn't a single cavern but a sprawling network of passages, chambers and vertical shafts, and crossing it takes a full day on foot. Corridors range from comfortably wide to barely shoulder-width, and stairs carved into the rock connect many levels. There are no street signs and no consistent logic; directions are given by landmark ("past the third waterfall chamber, below the olive press, take the descending passage marked by the red cloth"). Children learn the labyrinth by heart before adolescence. Visitors *will* get lost without a guide. The deepest levels are warmer, heated and faintly lit by geothermal vents, while the top levels near the greenhouse are the brightest, with shafts that let in natural daylight.

**Atmosphere:** The city is lit mainly by thousands of olive oil lamps in carved niches and on iron hooks, which cast warm, golden, uneven light with pools of shadow between them. The air stays cool and stable, regulated by the surrounding stone. Sound behaves strangely: distant conversations carry clearly through narrow passages, while nearby sounds vanish into vast chamber ceilings. It smells of burning olive oil, stone dust, cooking food, the stables and the green breath of the greenhouse carried down through the vents. It feels ancient in a way that's hard to put into words: not ruined, but heavy with accumulated time, as though the deeper chambers are quietly watching.

**The Greenhouse:** A series of interconnected growing chambers behind the glass panels, following the natural contours of the rock. Some are low and dense with plants, while others open into tall vaulted spaces where olive trees grow to full height. The glass is the only significant modern addition to the original structure. The greenhouse produces most of the city's food: olives above all, along with root vegetables, herbs, grains on carved terraces, and goats and chickens in sectioned-off stables.

**Water:** Ancient hand-carved aqueducts still carry fresh, cold water through the city, feeding pools and powering simple grain mills. Wastewater drains through separate channels that have worked for over two thousand years with minimal maintenance. Geothermal vents deep below heat the bathhouses.

**Around the City:**
- **Homes:** Chambers range from single rooms to large family warrens. Furniture is carved from stone and wood, personalized with textiles, drying herbs, carvings and the belongings of generations. Doorways are covered with curtains rather than closed.
- **Eating Houses:** Communal chambers with open hearths and low stone tables, serving whatever is in season.
- **Bathhouses:** Large chambers built around steaming geothermal pools, used for hygiene, socializing and relaxation, with no separation by gender or status.
- **Workshops and Art Spaces:** Chambers for weaving, carving, metalwork and repair, with walls throughout the city carved, painted and hung with textiles.
- **Clinics:** Staffed by practitioners of traditional Koumenese medicine, which is broadly comparable to ancient Egyptian medicine with selective modern additions (penicillin is available).
- **Other Services:** Brothels (treated as a normal social service), stables and simple, clean guest lodgings for people traveling across the city.

**The People:** Most Koumenese speak Ukrainian as their first language, with the elders keeping the old tongue alive. English is rare and usually spoken poorly, if at all. Modern modesty conventions don't apply here; nudity is unremarkable. Residents may ignore visitors at first, speaking only to their local escort as if the outsider isn't there. This isn't hostility, just cultural unfamiliarity, and it usually warms with time and demonstrated respect.

## City of Oribasaka
Oribasaka is the walled home city of the Oribasa draconid clan, with a population of roughly 3,000, nearly all of them Oribasa.

**Location:** The city occupies an island charted as Hjelmø (anglicized to "Hjelmo Island" in federal documents), though everyone who lives there calls it Oribasaka, roughly "home of the Oribasa" in the clan's dead tongue. It's about 22 square miles in the Virgin Islands group east of Puerto Rico, held as protected clan territory under US administration. A steep volcanic ridge runs down the island's spine; the windward side is uninhabited cliff, and everything else sits on the leeward slope. Getting to Weyland takes a five-hour flight to the mainland plus a cross-country leg: a full travel day each way.

The city is terraced up that slope above a working harbor, ringed by a stone wall that is four centuries old in places and patched with modern block everywhere else. There are two gates: the Harbor Gate at the bottom and the Hill Gate at the top.

Four miles across the channel lies the larger island that was Oribasa territory before colonization, with its deepwater harbor, arable plain and the clan's burial ground. The old harbor town is now a resort and marina. The burial ground sits inside a federal wildlife refuge that is closed to the public, including the Oribasa. They have petitioned for access for sixty years. On clear nights, the resort lights are visible from anywhere in the upper city.

**Atmosphere:** Hot, bright, salt-blown and loud, with trade winds, gulls, hammering from the forges, kids running up and down the terrace steps and everyone talking over everyone. It's thoroughly modern: electricity, cell phones and everything else. At first, it reads as an ordinary coastal town having a good day. Then a stranger walks in, a hundred slit-pupiled eyes register it at the same moment, and the volume drops by half. The warmth here is real, and it's reserved entirely for people who belong. It smells like salt, hot metal, frying oil and woodsmoke. The language is English; the old clan tongue survives only in names, ceremony, endearments and swearing.

**Layout:**
- **The Terraces:** Stepped market streets running down the slope, with awnings strung between buildings and stalls selling produce, fish, metalwork and secondhand everything. The heart of the city. Market days are Wednesday and Saturday.
- **The Matriarch's Hall:** Old stone at the high point of the city, with spiral ironwork over every window and the clan archive sealed in the back. It's the seat and residence of the matriarch. Its doors stand open in daylight, which is its own kind of statement, and nobody walks in anyway.
- **The Forges:** Four family-held smithies producing the metalwork and glasswork the Oribasa were famous for a thousand years ago, and are quietly famous for now. Everything has spiral patterning, and the smiths are the closest thing the city has to celebrities.
- **The Harbor:** Fishing boats, a small commercial dock and a ferry to the mainland twice a day. It's the only way onto the island without a boat, and the whole harbor watches the ferry come in.
- **The Outer Holdings:** Farms and pasture above the Hill Gate, where the clan's food comes from.

**Fashion and Dress:** Oribasaka is a modern American town. Jeans, t-shirts, work boots, sundresses, sneakers and band shirts are completely normal, especially among people under forty. Full traditional dress (layered fabrics with handcrafted metal clasps, plates and clan spiralwork) is the formal end of the scale, signaling status, office or occasion. Elders wear it often, leadership always wears it, and everyone else owns one set that they wear about four times a year. What *is* universal is a single clan-made piece worn with whatever else you have on: a spiralwork pendant, a belt buckle, a cuff or a clasp. Horn ornaments are the clearest status markers, with bands, cuffs and wraps fitted to the horn, cheap steel for most and clan-gold for a few. A teenager might have one thin band and be proud of it.

Every garment made in Oribasaka has a tail vent, cut and reinforced for the wearer. Off-island clothes have to be modified, which supports a small tailoring trade near the Harbor Gate, and an unaltered pair of jeans is a dead giveaway that someone is new, visiting or doesn't have a tail. Draconids run warm in an already hot climate, so light fabrics, bare arms and open collars are standard. The one exception is the back of the neck; within the clan, the mark is unremarkable, since everyone has one and nobody looks.

Almost everyone has horns and a draconid tail. Some draconids live in town with non-draconid spouses and children, so non-draconids aren't unheard of. That used to be controversial but has quieted with newer generations, though the clan's leadership is held to an unapologetically high standard of carrying on the draconid lineage.

**Hurricanes:** Hurricane season runs from June through November, and the outer holdings take damage most years. Federal disaster aid is turned away at the Harbor Gate as a matter of course. The clan rebuilds itself, slowly, and considers the delay better than the debt.

**Outsiders:** The Oribasa rarely receive visitors. A new person inside the walls, especially a non-draconid, is watched constantly, served politely, charged fairly and spoken to as little as possible. Nobody is cruel. Nobody is warm, either.
